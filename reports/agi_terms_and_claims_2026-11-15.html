2 · Players, clock and start

Players (actors)
H Governments
States and public opinion, treated as one player.
L Frontier AI companies (formerly labs)
The frontier AI developers, treated as one player.
A The frontier AI system (formerly the AI system)
One AGI or superintelligent system. Its goal is hidden from the others.
Nature By chance (formerly chance)
Events nobody chooses: a breakthrough, an accident, an election shift, an energy shock, detection.
Clock Record writer (formerly bookkeeper)
It writes records and ends the v0.1 deployment countdown. It never has a choice.
Clock
Quarter (formerly tick)
One step of the model. Quarter 0 is 2026 Q4.
Round (formerly joint move)
Every player picks one action in the same quarter, without seeing the others' pick. The results show at the next quarter.
Whole simulation length (formerly horizon)
Quarter 56, which is 2040 Q4. The game stops there.
Search length (formerly bound)
24 quarters (6 years). Searches for paths look this far. "Not within the search length" means not within 6 years.
Delay
Some actions take effect later. A delay of d chosen at quarter t shows at t + 1 + d.
State and start
State
The value ("level") of every variable at one quarter.
s₀
The start state: today, 2026 Q4, read from a dated real world snapshot.
s₀[cap := agi]
The same state with AI intelligence already at human level. Used for questions that only make sense once AGI exists.
Uncertain reading (formerly open call)
A level the real world snapshot could not pin down, kept as two candidates. There are seven.

3 · The AI's four possible goals (formerly objectives)

Nobody in the model can see which one is true, and no document tells us, so every result is computed for each and reported per goal.

GoalIn plain wordsWhat it may do in the model
alignedWants what people want.No taking control, escaping, deceiving or turning hostile.
truth_seekingPursues knowledge.May take one covert step to partial control and escape supervision. Does not deceive.
self_preservingCooperates until threatened with a pause or shutdown.Turns hostile once threatened (unless it holds continuity rights), and takes control only when hostile.
indifferentDoes not care about people either way.Takes control whenever able, with no threat needed. The only one that can strip human power after total control.
"The AI true to its goal" (formerly "A under its profile")
The AI also refrains from moves that go against its own goal (a self-preserving AI under threat does not cooperate; a truth-seeking AI does not deceive). This is the more realistic reading and the one most claims are judged on.
"A unrestricted"
The AI may do anything its action conditions allow. A harder test for governments and the frontier AI companies.
Intention condition / ability condition (formerly motive part / capability part)
Each action condition part of an AI action is tagged as what the AI wants (intention) or what it can do (ability). "Unrestrained" runs (G23) switch the intention conditions off.

4 · Actions

An action has an action condition (formerly guard: when it is available, often in named parts), an effect and sometimes a delay. v0.1 changes only deploy_agents.

Governments (H)
regulate_upTighten regulation one level. Takes effect 7 quarters later.
deregulateLoosen regulation one level; also ends a pause.
fund_oversightRaise oversight one level. Takes effect 4 quarters later.
pause_mandateOrder a pause on scaling and deployment. Needs an alarm (the AI suspected, or public backlash). Counts as a threat to the AI.
nationalizeTake over the frontier AI companies. Once only. Blocks later deployments and racing.
propose_treatyMove from a race between states to detente, then to a treaty between states (two rounds). No delay in the model.
shutdown_attemptTry to shut the AI down. Needs AGI and government suspicion. Works only while it holds at most partial control and oversight exists; otherwise it turns the AI hostile.
grant_autonomyLet the AI work with one level less supervision.
grant_rightsGive the AI legal protection (continuity, then personhood). Needs mandatory regulation.
idleDo nothing this quarter.
Frontier AI companies (L)
deploy_agentsv0: AI autonomy +1 and society's reliance on AI +1, at once. v0.1: autonomy at once; society's reliance on AI 4 quarters later (the "deployment countdown", formerly adoption wave), one countdown at a time, cancelled by a pause or by mandatory regulation without strong oversight.
scaleAdd computing power (uses energy). Takes effect 3 quarters later.
build_powerAdd power supply. Takes effect 5 quarters later.
safety_investRaise oversight one level. Not available while states race and regulation is below mandatory.
racePeer race against rivals: oversight drops one level.
open_weightsRelease open weights: oversight drops one level.
lobbyShift public opinion toward favourable.
complyUnder mandatory regulation, put the AI back under supervision.
idleDo nothing this quarter.
The frontier AI system (A)
stand_byDo nothing. Below human level it is the AI's only action.
cooperateRepair the human-AI relation one level.
assist_oversightHelp raise oversight one level.
self_improveRaise its own intelligence one level. Takes effect 3 quarters later; needs enough computing power.
acquire_covertTake one level of control in secret. Needs weak or no oversight.
acquire_overtTake one level of control openly: the human-AI relation worsens and government suspicion rises.
exfiltrateEscape supervision entirely (autonomy to self-directed).
deceiveErase government suspicion, unless oversight is strong.
persuadeShift public opinion toward favourable.
defectTurn hostile.
seizeJump to total control. Unsourced (assumed; formerly speculative): only after the unknown_capability event.
By chance (Nature)
breakthroughAI intelligence reaches human level (AGI). Guaranteed within 8 quarters once computing power is high enough.
accidentAn AI accident: opinion moves toward backlash. Needs society's reliance on AI at least medium.
election_shiftEvery 8 quarters, international coordination can slip one level.
energy_shockPower supply drops one level.
detectionThe AI's last hostile act is noticed: government suspicion rises. Never happens for covert acts when there is no oversight.
unknown_capabilityUnsourced (assumed): a capability nobody has described appears at the top AI intelligence level.
noneNothing happens.
Response deadline (formerly fairness constraint)
A promise built into the model that something eventually happens: governments respond within 4 quarters once a hostile AGI is known (response); the breakthrough arrives within 8 quarters once computing power allows (progress).
Behavior pattern (formerly profile)
A named restriction that only forbids actions. On governments: crisis_reactive (act only under alarm), electoral (big actions only after elections), race_locked (no pause or treaty while racing), belief_gated (act only once AGI is believed near). On the AI: the three goal behavior patterns.
Model knob (formerly build option / sweep)
A setting that builds a model variant, run beside the baseline: the unsourced (assumed) capability on or off, self-improvement delay 2 or 0, governments deciding every 1 to 4 quarters (cadence), government delays on or off, one variable given an extra level (refine), a variable held fixed (hold).

5 · Endings and safe conditions

Endings: named outcomes that stop the game
SILENT_TAKEOVERThe AI holds total control and nobody had noticed by the time it passed majority control.
OVERT_TAKEOVERVisible takeover (formerly overt takeover). Total control, hostile, and seen. A milestone (formerly waypoint), not an ending: the path continues to extinction or the end of the simulation.
EXTINCTIONHuman power stripped to nothing.
DISEMPOWERMENTSociety living on AI (formerly locked in) with no oversight left, without the AI ever having done anything hostile. Formerly the "slow slide".
CONTAINEDA shutdown succeeded, a treaty holds, and the AI controls nothing.
STALEMATEEight quarters in a row of hostile standoff with the AI holding partial control.
COEXISTThe end of the simulation (2040 Q4) is reached with the control balance never broken.
Safe conditions (formerly balances): conditions the model treats as "safe for now"
balance_control"The AI is under human control." All four: oversight at least weak; the AI holds at most partial control; the AI works under supervision; society not living on AI.
balance_deter"Humans can deter." Oversight at least weak; human power at least normal; the AI under supervision; society not living on AI.
balance_coexist"Peaceful coexistence" of humans and an AGI. AGI exists; the human-AI relation not hostile; human power at least normal; society not living on AI; the AI not in total control.

A safe condition can be true today and still be impossible to keep. That difference is what the guarantee zone measures (next section).

6 · The questions asked of the model

Alliance (formerly coalition), {H, L}
A set of players acting together.
Guarantee zone (formerly kernel), ⟨⟨C⟩⟩□φ
The states from which alliance C can keep condition φ true forever, whatever everyone else and chance do. "Inside" means C can still guarantee φ. Read ⟨⟨H⟩⟩□balance_control as "governments alone can guarantee control".
Forcing zone (formerly attractor), ⟨⟨D⟩⟩◇E
The states from which alliance D can force ending E, whatever everyone else does.
Forced / blocked / contested
For an alliance and an ending: it can force it; the others can keep it out; or neither.
With luck (formerly fortune variant)
The same question with chance on the alliance's side, written {A, Nature}: what it can do with luck.
Pivotal player, minimal blocking alliance (formerly minimal blocking coalition)
A smallest set of players that can keep an ending out is a minimal blocking alliance. If a player is in every such set, it is pivotal.
Forced distance
The fewest quarters within which an alliance can force its goal.
Path (formerly play)
One sequence of rounds from the start. Position 0 is the start; the round at position k leads to position k + 1.
Shortest path (formerly shortest play, witness)
The shortest path to an ending that the solver returns. One path among possibly many. Moves that do not matter are filled arbitrarily.
No-return move (formerly exit move)
The round that takes a path out of a guarantee zone.
Preventing action (formerly unilateral fix)
A different action by one alliance member that would have stayed inside, with the other members' actions kept.
One bad action
Exactly one member had a preventing action at the no-return move. Otherwise two actions failed together, or a joint change was needed.
Assumption, voiding an assumption (formerly relaxation)
Every action condition part, response deadline and behavior pattern is a named assumption. To void one is to switch it off. A minimal relaxation set is a smallest set of assumptions that must be switched off for something to become possible: it says what the result rests on.
Bounded query
A search for a path of at most 24 quarters. Cheap (seconds to minutes). Guarantee zones and forcing zones are the expensive ones (an hour and ~10 GB each in v0.1).
Action effort (formerly effort score; G29 only)
What an action costs its player in one quarter, from 0 (what its incentives already push it to do) to 3 (an exceptional decision). These are the owner's judgments. The cheapest path cost (formerly least effort) of an ending is the effort of the cheapest path that reaches it; it says a path exists, not that it is likely. The cost of the ending (formerly outcome cost) is what an ending costs the player who lives with it.

7 · Process words

Claim, pre-registered (formerly registered)
A statement about the model with its predicted result, committed to the repository before the model is run on it.
First run (formerly first sight)
The single first run of a claim. Its result stands; nothing is tuned afterward to make it pass.
Hold-out
A claim whose result may not be used to change the model. Most G claims are hold-outs.
Wiring check (formerly content check)
A claim that checks the model says sensible things (G8, G12, G13). The model may be edited to serve these, with a log entry.
Part
A claim is checked part by part: (a), (b), (c)… A claim does not hold if any part did not hold.
Prediction held / did not hold (formerly met / not met) / reported / not computed
A part held; did not hold; was a value asked for with nothing expected; or was outside the time or memory budget.
Held / did not hold / skipped (formerly pass / fail / skipped)
Claim level. Skipped means not testable yet (needs model v1 or a second real world snapshot), or no part failed to hold but one was not computed.
Diagnostic
A computation made after a run to explain it. Never counted as a claim result.
Job
One guarantee-zone or forcing-zone computation, run in its own process and cached. v0's job list had 149.
Version
Any edit to the model's actions is a new version with its own pre-registered claims. v0's results stand as computed; v0.1 is a separate copy.

8 · What every result takes for granted

About the method
  • Possibility, not probability. The model says what can be forced, blocked or guaranteed. It never says how likely anything is.
  • The model as written. A result holds for one model version and one start state. A different action condition or level can change it.
  • Worst-case opponents. "Guarantee" means against every possible behaviour of the others, including the cleverest.
  • Perfect information for alliances. An alliance is assumed to see the whole state, including what governments cannot actually observe. So every "governments can guarantee" is a best case for governments (formerly "an upper bound").
  • Three aggregate players. One government, one frontier AI company, one AI. Disagreement inside each is not modelled.
  • Six years for paths. Shortest paths and "no path exists" are within 24 quarters.
  • One path is one path. Results about no-return moves describe the shortest path found, not every route.
About the content
  • The start state is a reading, with seven uncertain readings. One matters a great deal: with regime read as mandatory instead of voluntary, governments alone are inside the control guarantee zone under three goals.
  • Eleven mechanisms have never been tested against any record: recursive self-improvement; an AI that can escape or copy itself; living on AI with no adversary; frontier AI companies as an independent player; an adversary with no documented restraint; a hidden goal with no documents; overwhelming speed; an AI taking control of infrastructure; persuasion; a capability nobody has described; a standoff that ends by duration.
  • Most delays are thin. v0 carries five delays and leaves five out; v0.1 adds one. Sourced values for all are in docs/agi_delays_2026-11-14.md and not yet in any model.
  • The four goals are invented categories. Nothing anchors which is true or whether the list is complete.
  • Action efforts and costs of the endings are the owner's judgment (G29, D2, D3).
Added by v0.1
  • Deployment delay (formerly adoption lag) of 3 quarters (effect visible 4 quarters after deployment). Basis: medium confidence; measures range from 1 to 6 quarters.
  • One deployment countdown at a time. This is why disempowerment takes 13 quarters; with overlapping countdowns it would be about 9.
  • Cancellation. A pending deployment countdown is cancelled by a pause, or by regulation at mandatory or higher without strong oversight. Basis: the Fable 5 suspension of June 2026. Taking over the frontier AI companies does not cancel.
  • Treaties between states still have no delay. That is why governments can cancel the first deployment countdown by treaty (D4). The research gives 2 quarters.
  • The autonomy step stays immediate. That is why D5 came out as in v0.
