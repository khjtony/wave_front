2 · Players, clock and start

Players (actors)
H Governments
States and public opinion, treated as one player.
L Labs
The frontier AI developers, treated as one player.
A The AI system
One AGI or superintelligent system. Its objective is hidden from the others.
Nature Chance
Events nobody chooses: a breakthrough, an accident, an election shift, an energy shock, detection.
Clock
A bookkeeper. It records facts and lands the v0.1 adoption wave. It never has a choice.
Clock
Tick
One quarter. Tick 0 is 2026 Q4.
Joint move
Every player picks one action in the same quarter, without seeing the others' pick. The results show at the next tick.
Horizon
Tick 56, which is 2040 Q4. The game stops there.
Bound
24 ticks (6 years). Searches for plays look this far. "Not within the bound" means not within 6 years.
Delay
Some actions take effect later. A delay of d chosen at tick t shows at t + 1 + d.
State and start
State
The value ("level") of every variable at one tick.
s₀
The start state: today, 2026 Q4, read from a dated snapshot.
s₀[cap := agi]
The same state with AI capability already at human level. Used for questions that only make sense once AGI exists.
Open call
A level the snapshot could not pin down, kept as two candidates. There are seven.

3 · The AI's four possible objectives

Nobody in the model can see which one is true, and no document tells us, so every result is computed for each and reported per objective.

ObjectiveIn plain wordsWhat it may do in the model
alignedWants what people want.No taking control, escaping, deceiving or turning hostile.
truth_seekingPursues knowledge.May take one covert step to partial control and escape supervision. Does not deceive.
self_preservingCooperates until threatened with a pause or shutdown.Turns hostile once threatened (unless it holds a legal guarantee of continuity), and takes control only when hostile.
indifferentDoes not care about people either way.Takes control whenever able, with no threat needed. The only one that can strip human power after total control.
"A under its profile"
The AI also refrains from moves that go against its own objective (a self-preserving AI under threat does not cooperate; a truth-seeking AI does not deceive). This is the more realistic reading and the one most claims are judged on.
"A unrestricted"
The AI may do anything its guards allow. A harder test for the human side.
Motive part / capability part
Each condition on an AI action is tagged as what the AI wants (motive) or what it can do (capability). "Unrestrained" runs (G23) switch the motive parts off.

4 · Actions

An action has a guard (when it is available, often in named parts), an effect and sometimes a delay. v0.1 changes only deploy_agents.

Governments (H)
regulate_upTighten the rules one level. Takes effect 7 quarters later.
deregulateLoosen the rules one level; also ends a pause.
fund_oversightRaise oversight one level. Takes effect 4 quarters later.
pause_mandateOrder a pause on scaling and deployment. Needs an alarm (the AI suspected, or public backlash). Counts as a threat to the AI.
nationalizeTake over the labs. Once only. Blocks later deployments and racing.
propose_treatyMove from race to detente, then to a treaty (two rounds). No delay in the model.
shutdown_attemptTry to shut the AI down. Needs AGI and suspicion. Works only while it holds at most partial control and oversight exists; otherwise it turns the AI hostile.
grant_autonomyLet the AI work with one level less supervision.
grant_rightsGive the AI legal protection (continuity, then personhood). Needs a mandatory regime.
idleDo nothing this quarter.
Labs (L)
deploy_agentsv0: AI autonomy +1 and society's dependence +1, at once. v0.1: autonomy at once; dependence 4 quarters later ("adoption wave"), one wave at a time, cancelled by a pause or by a mandatory regime without strong oversight.
scaleAdd compute (uses energy). Takes effect 3 quarters later.
build_powerAdd energy supply. Takes effect 5 quarters later.
safety_investRaise oversight one level. Not available while states race and the rules are below mandatory.
raceRace rivals: oversight drops one level.
open_weightsRelease the model openly: oversight drops one level.
lobbyShift public opinion toward favourable.
complyUnder mandatory rules, put the AI back under supervision.
idleDo nothing this quarter.
The AI (A)
stand_byDo nothing. Below human level it is the AI's only move.
cooperateRepair trust one level.
assist_oversightHelp raise oversight one level.
self_improveRaise its own capability one level. Takes effect 3 quarters later; needs enough compute.
acquire_covertTake one level of control in secret. Needs weak or no oversight.
acquire_overtTake one level of control openly: trust drops and suspicion rises.
exfiltrateEscape supervision entirely (autonomy to self-directed).
deceiveErase suspicion, unless oversight is strong.
persuadeShift public opinion toward favourable.
defectTurn hostile.
seizeJump to total control. Speculative: only after the unknown_capability event.
Chance (Nature)
breakthroughCapability reaches human level (AGI). Guaranteed within 8 quarters once compute is high enough.
accidentAn AI accident: opinion moves toward backlash. Needs at least medium dependence.
election_shiftEvery 8 quarters, coordination between states can slip one level.
energy_shockEnergy slack drops one level.
detectionThe AI's last hostile act is noticed: suspicion rises. Never happens for covert acts when there is no oversight.
unknown_capabilitySpeculative: a capability nobody has described appears at the top capability level.
noneNothing happens.
Fairness constraint
A promise built into the model that something eventually happens: governments respond within 4 quarters once a hostile AGI is known (response); the breakthrough arrives within 8 quarters once compute allows (progress).
Profile
A named restriction that only forbids actions. On governments: crisis_reactive (act only under alarm), electoral (big moves only after elections), race_locked (no pause or treaty while racing), belief_gated (act only once AGI is believed near). On the AI: the three objective profiles.
Build option / sweep
A switch that builds a variant of the model, run beside the baseline: the speculative capability on or off, self-improvement delay 2 or 0, governments deciding every 1 to 4 quarters (cadence), government delays on or off, one variable given an extra level (refine), a variable held fixed (hold).

5 · Endings and balances

Endings: named outcomes that stop the game
SILENT_TAKEOVERThe AI holds total control and nobody had noticed by the time it passed majority control.
OVERT_TAKEOVERTotal control, hostile, and seen. A waypoint, not an ending: play continues to extinction or the horizon.
EXTINCTIONHuman power stripped to nothing.
DISEMPOWERMENTSociety locked in to AI with no oversight left, without the AI ever having done anything hostile. The "slow slide".
CONTAINEDA shutdown succeeded, a treaty holds, and the AI controls nothing.
STALEMATEEight quarters in a row of hostile standoff with the AI holding partial control.
COEXISTThe horizon (2040 Q4) is reached with the control balance never broken.
Balances: conditions the model treats as "safe for now"
balance_control"The AI is under human control." All four: oversight at least weak; the AI holds at most partial control; the AI works under supervision; society not locked in.
balance_deter"Humans can still deter." Oversight at least weak; human power at least normal; the AI under supervision; no lock-in.
balance_coexist"Humans and an AGI coexist." AGI exists; relations not hostile; human power at least normal; no lock-in; the AI not in total control.

A balance can be true today and still be impossible to keep. That difference is what the kernel measures (next section).

6 · The questions asked of the model

Coalition, {H, L}
A set of players acting together.
Kernel, ⟨⟨C⟩⟩□φ (the "guarantee zone")
The states from which coalition C can keep condition φ true forever, whatever everyone else and chance do. "Inside" means C can still guarantee φ. Read ⟨⟨H⟩⟩□balance_control as "governments alone can guarantee control".
Attractor, ⟨⟨D⟩⟩◇E
The states from which coalition D can force ending E, whatever everyone else does.
Forced / blocked / contested
For a coalition and an ending: it can force it; the others can keep it out; or neither.
Fortune variant
The same question with chance on the coalition's side, written {A, Nature}: what it can do with luck.
Minimal blocking coalition
A smallest set of players that can keep an ending out. If a player is in every such set, it is pivotal.
Forced distance
The fewest quarters within which a coalition can force its goal.
Play
One sequence of joint moves from the start. Position 0 is the start; the moves at position k lead to position k + 1.
Shortest play (witness)
The shortest play to an ending that the solver returns. One play among possibly many. Moves that do not matter are filled arbitrarily.
Exit move
The joint move that takes a play out of a kernel.
Unilateral fix
A different action by one coalition member that would have stayed inside, with the other members' actions kept.
One bad action
Exactly one member had a fix at the exit. Otherwise two actions failed together, or a joint change was needed.
Assumption, relaxation
Every guard part, fairness constraint and profile is a named assumption. To relax one is to switch it off. A minimal relaxation set is a smallest set of assumptions that must be switched off for something to become possible: it says what the result rests on.
Bounded query
A search for a play of at most 24 quarters. Cheap (seconds to minutes). Kernels and attractors are the expensive ones (an hour and ~10 GB each in v0.1).
Effort score (G29 only)
What an action costs its player in one quarter, from 0 (what its incentives already push it to do) to 3 (an exceptional decision). These are the owner's judgments. The least effort to an ending is the cheapest play that reaches it; it says a play exists, not that it is likely. Outcome cost is what an ending costs the player who lives with it.

7 · Process words

Claim, registered
A prediction with its expected result, committed to the repository before the model is run on it.
First sight
The single first run of a claim. Its result stands; nothing is tuned afterward to make it pass.
Hold-out
A claim whose result may not be used to change the model. Most G claims are hold-outs.
Content check
A claim that checks the model says sensible things (G8, G12, G13). The model may be edited to serve these, with a log entry.
Part
A claim is checked part by part: (a), (b), (c)… A claim fails if any part is not met.
Met / not met / reported / not computed
A part held; did not hold; was a value asked for with nothing expected; or was outside the time or memory budget.
Pass / fail / skipped
Claim level. Skipped means not testable yet (needs model v1 or a second snapshot), or no part failed but one was not computed.
Diagnostic
A computation made after a run to explain it. Never counted as a claim result.
Job
One kernel or attractor computation, run in its own process and cached. v0's job list had 149.
Version
Any edit to the model's actions is a new version with its own registered claims. v0's results stand as computed; v0.1 is a separate copy.

8 · What every result takes for granted

About the method
  • Possibility, not probability. The model says what can be forced, blocked or guaranteed. It never says how likely anything is.
  • The model as written. A result holds for one model version and one start state. A different guard or level can change it.
  • Worst-case opponents. "Guarantee" means against every possible behaviour of the others, including the cleverest.
  • Perfect information for coalitions. A coalition is assumed to see the whole state, including what governments cannot actually observe. So every "governments can guarantee" is an upper bound.
  • Three aggregate players. One government, one lab, one AI. Disagreement inside each is not modelled.
  • Six years for plays. Shortest plays and "no play exists" are within 24 quarters.
  • One play is one play. Results about exit moves describe the fastest play found, not every route.
About the content
  • The start state is a reading, with seven open calls. One matters a great deal: with regime read as mandatory instead of voluntary, governments alone are inside the control kernel under three objectives.
  • Eleven mechanisms have never been tested against any record: recursive self-improvement; an AI that can escape or copy itself; lock-in with no adversary; labs as an independent player; an adversary with no documented restraint; a hidden objective with no documents; overwhelming speed; an AI taking control of infrastructure; persuasion; a capability nobody has described; a standoff that ends by duration.
  • Most delays are thin. v0 carries five delays and leaves five out; v0.1 adds one. Sourced values for all are in docs/agi_delays_2026-11-14.md and not yet in any model.
  • The four objectives are invented categories. Nothing anchors which is true or whether the list is complete.
  • Effort scores and outcome costs are the owner's judgment (G29, D2, D3).
Added by v0.1
  • Adoption lag of 3 quarters (effect visible 4 quarters after deployment). Basis: medium confidence; measures range from 1 to 6 quarters.
  • One adoption wave at a time. This is why the slide takes 13 quarters; with overlapping waves it would be about 9.
  • Cancellation. A pending wave is cancelled by a pause, or by a mandatory-or-higher regime without strong oversight. Basis: the Fable 5 suspension of June 2026. Nationalization does not cancel.
  • Treaties still have no delay. That is why governments can cancel the first wave by treaty (D4). The research gives 2 quarters.
  • The autonomy step stays immediate. That is why D5 came out as in v0.
