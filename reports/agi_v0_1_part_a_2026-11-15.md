## What the results say

1. **The lag slows the slide by a factor of two, and does not price it (D1, D2).** The slide into `DISEMPOWERMENT` now takes 13 quarters instead of 6 and still costs `H` and `L` nothing on the effort scores of record. 13 and not 12: the second wave needs `cap ≥ agi` (the `capable` part of `deploy_agents`), which arrives one quarter after the first wave lands, so the shortest play deploys at ticks 0, 5 and 9 and lands at 4, 9 and 13. This rests on the modelling choice of one pending wave at a time (docs/agi_v0_1.md section 2); with overlapping waves the figure would be about 9.

2. **The lag does not slow the silent takeover (D3 (a), not met).** Under `indifferent` the shortest `SILENT_TAKEOVER` is 7 quarters in v0 and in v0.1. The takeover runs on the autonomy step, which the owner's decision keeps immediate, and does not wait on `dependence` at all. On the play found (replayed on the explicit model), `H.grant_autonomy` and `L.deploy_agents` in the same quarter (tick 2) take `a_autonomy` from `supervised` to `self_directed` at tick 3; `self_directed` satisfies the `reach` condition of the acquisitions without any level of `dependence`, so `A` acquires at ticks 4, 5 and 6 with `dependence` still `low` until the wave lands at tick 6, and `a_control = total` with the system unseen at tick 7. The drafting prediction assumed the takeover waited on dependence; it does not. Of the three results of v0 that section 1 of the registration says rest on deployment speed, the lag moves one (the slide) and leaves the takeover where it was.

3. **Governments can cancel the first wave, by treaty (D4).** The claim reasoned that a pause needs an alarm and a mandatory regime takes 7 quarters, so nothing stops a wave started at tick 0. The model has a third route: `H.propose_treaty` raises `regime` to `treaty` within two quarters with no delay (v0 does not carry the treaty's lag), and a treaty without strong oversight fails the deployment's licence, which cancels the pending wave (on the play found, regime `treaty` and oversight `none` at tick 3, the wave cancelled before it would land at tick 4). D4 (b) finds the same route at tick 2. This result depends on a lag v0 does not carry: the delay research gives `H.propose_treaty` 2 quarters (docs/agi_delays_2026-11-14.md, the Bletchley declaration), and with it the treaty would land after the wave. It is the strongest reason to bring the treaty lag in next.

4. **What did not change.** The autonomy step still lands in the quarter of the deployment, so a deployment at tick 0 breaks `balance_control` at tick 1 as in v0; D5 (the kernel claim) is expected not to be met for that reason (docs/agi_v0_1.md section 5).

## The harness defect in D4 (a), and its rerun

The first-sight query of D4 (a) asserted that the transitions into ticks 1 to 3 were taken but not the one into tick 4 (`active[4]`; in `wavefront.bmc` the flag at tick t gates the transition into t). The state at tick 4 was therefore unconstrained, and the solver set `dependence = low` there although the play it returned lands the wave (replayed on the explicit model: `dependence = med` at tick 4). The four "not met" of the first sight are recorded as computed in `reports/agi_v0_1_parts_first_sight.jsonl`. The query was fixed (`tests/agi_v0_1/claims.py`, `first_wave_cancelled`: `active[1..4]`), D4 alone was run again (`reports/agi_v0_1_d4_rerun.log`, `reports/agi_v0_1_parts_d4_rerun.jsonl`), and every play of the rerun was replayed on the explicit model: (a) is still not met under every objective, by the treaty route above; (b) is unchanged. No guard, effect or claim text changed.
