## What the results say

1. **The deployment delay slows disempowerment by a factor of two, and does not price it (D1, D2).** The path into `DISEMPOWERMENT` now takes 13 quarters instead of 6 and still costs `H` and `L` nothing on the action efforts of record. 13 and not 12: the second deployment countdown needs `cap ≥ agi` (the `capable` action condition part of `deploy_agents`), which arrives one quarter after the first countdown ends, so the shortest path deploys at quarters 0, 5 and 9 and its countdowns end at 4, 9 and 13. This rests on the modelling choice of one pending deployment countdown at a time (docs/agi_v0_1.md section 2); with overlapping countdowns the figure would be about 9.

2. **The deployment delay does not slow the silent takeover (D3 (a), prediction did not hold).** Under `indifferent` the shortest `SILENT_TAKEOVER` is 7 quarters in v0 and in v0.1. The takeover runs on the autonomy step, which the owner's decision keeps immediate, and does not wait on `dependence` at all. On the path found (replayed on the explicit model), `H.grant_autonomy` and `L.deploy_agents` in the same quarter (quarter 2) take `a_autonomy` from `supervised` to `self_directed` at quarter 3; `self_directed` satisfies the `reach` action condition part of the acquisitions without any level of `dependence`, so `A` acquires at quarters 4, 5 and 6 with `dependence` still `low` until the deployment countdown ends at quarter 6, and `a_control = total` with the frontier AI system unseen at quarter 7. The drafting prediction assumed the takeover waited on society's reliance on AI; it does not. Of the three results of v0 that section 1 of the pre-registration says rest on deployment speed, the deployment delay moves one (disempowerment) and leaves the takeover where it was.

3. **Governments can cancel the first deployment countdown, by treaty (D4).** The claim reasoned that a pause needs an alarm and mandatory regulation takes 7 quarters, so nothing stops a deployment countdown started at quarter 0. The model has a third route: `H.propose_treaty` raises `regime` to `treaty` within two quarters with no delay (v0 does not carry the treaty's delay), and a treaty without strong oversight fails the deployment permit, which cancels the pending countdown (on the path found, regulation strictness `treaty` and oversight `none` at quarter 3, the deployment cancelled before its countdown would end at quarter 4). D4 (b) finds the same route at quarter 2. This result depends on a delay v0 does not carry: the delay research gives `H.propose_treaty` 2 quarters (docs/agi_delays_2026-11-14.md, the Bletchley declaration), and with it the treaty would take effect after the countdown ends. It is the strongest reason to bring the treaty delay in next.

4. **What did not change.** The autonomy step still takes effect in the quarter of the deployment, so a deployment at quarter 0 breaks `balance_control` at quarter 1 as in v0; D5 (the guarantee zone claim) is expected not to hold for that reason (docs/agi_v0_1.md section 5).

## The test code defect in D4 (a), and its rerun

The first-run query of D4 (a) asserted that the transitions into quarters 1 to 3 were taken but not the one into quarter 4 (`active[4]`; in `wavefront.bmc` the flag at quarter t gates the transition into t). The state at quarter 4 was therefore unconstrained, and the solver set `dependence = low` there although on the path it returned the deployment countdown ends (replayed on the explicit model: `dependence = med` at quarter 4). The four "did not hold" of the first run are recorded as computed in `reports/agi_v0_1_parts_first_sight.jsonl`. The query was fixed (`tests/agi_v0_1/claims.py`, `first_wave_cancelled`: `active[1..4]`), D4 alone was run again (`reports/agi_v0_1_d4_rerun.log`, `reports/agi_v0_1_parts_d4_rerun.jsonl`), and every path of the rerun was replayed on the explicit model: (a) still does not hold under every AI goal, by the treaty route above; (b) is unchanged. No action condition, effect or claim text changed.
