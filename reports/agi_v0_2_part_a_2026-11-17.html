Open weights: an aligned AI is no longer a safe start

The first results of v0.2, the model that adds what an open-weight release does. All the cheap claims came out as predicted. The one that matters: if the public can retrain an open system, a silent takeover becomes possible in 7 quarters even when the AI starts out aligned. Without that consequence there is no such road within 6 years.

These are searches for plays within 24 quarters. They show what the model's rules allow, not what is likely. The kernel claims, which say what can be guaranteed, have not been run yet.

E6 · The road from an aligned start to a silent takeover

The fastest road the solver finds, quarter by quarter. Two chance events carry it: someone outside the labs releases open weights, and the next quarter the open system is retrained so that it no longer cares about people. After that it is the same road as for an AI that started indifferent.

Moves that do not matter for reaching the ending are filled with arbitrary legal ones. Read the outlined cells and the AI's row.

Why 7, the same as "indifferent"
The AI can do nothing before it reaches human level, which takes until 2027 Q4 on the fastest road. The release and the retraining fit inside that wait, so starting aligned costs the takeover no time.
What the labs and governments did
Nothing hostile and nothing unusual. Governments granted autonomy twice; the labs deployed agents once. Nobody suspects the AI until the quarter in which the takeover is complete.
What this rests on
That stripping safety training from an open model is cheap and fast is well sourced. That the result is an AI with a different objective, and that the one system in the model stands for the worst copy in circulation, are assumptions.

What this does and does not show

  • E6 (a) is the finding of this part. In v0 and v0.1, "aligned" and "truth-seeking" had no road to a silent takeover within 6 years. With retraining on, both do, and as fast as the worst objective. Whether any guarantee is lost is E6 (b) and E5, which need kernels.
  • E3 (a) could hardly have failed. Race pressure only removes options from the labs, and removing options cannot shorten the shortest road. Its real test is E3 (b).
  • E2 (a) and (c) are existence checks. They confirm the rule works as written: a pause no longer stops a pending wave once weights are open, while a treaty still does. E2 (b) says no recall does not slow or speed the slide.
  • E1 is a wiring check. With every consequence off, v0.2 behaves as v0.1 on every number compared.
  • Not covered: a second AI system outside the labs (model v1), and the treaty delay (v0.3).
