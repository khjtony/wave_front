Each bad ending has its own point of no return, and its own fix

For every bad ending, the last moment at which governments and labs can still guarantee to avoid it, read on one million random plays with a self-preserving AI. A treaty proposal is the commonest fix for the silent takeover and the stalemate; for the overt takeover it is a shutdown attempt. In all three, ordering a pause is the move that most often crosses the line.

Exploratory, not a registered claim and not a probability. Every player picks uniformly at random, so these are shares of random plays. v0.1 has none of the open-weight consequences of v0.2.

Reading across the three

  • A pause is the commonest harmful move in every one: 27% of the silent-takeover moments, 36% of the overt ones, 58% of the stalemate ones. In the model a pause is a threat to a self-preserving AI and takes nothing from one that is already self-directed. That explanation is my reading of the rules, not a computed result.
  • The fix depends on the ending. Before a silent takeover the situation is usually calm and the fixes are a treaty or safety investment. Before an overt one, half the moments already have a hostile AI with partial control and known hostility under strong oversight, and the fix is a shutdown attempt, which works 99% of the times it is available.
  • It is nearly always the governments' move. They alone held the fix at 60% of the silent-takeover moments, 82% of the overt ones and 98% of the stalemate ones.
  • The treaty's high rank rests on v0.1 giving treaties no delay, as in the earlier censuses.

Method: 1,000,000 uniform random plays to 2040 Q4, seed 20261117; kernels ⟨⟨H, L⟩⟩□¬E for each ending E, computed once and stored (overt takeover 131 min and 18 GB; the others 6 minutes or less). A fix is another action of one of the two which keeps every successor inside the kernel whatever the AI and chance play.
