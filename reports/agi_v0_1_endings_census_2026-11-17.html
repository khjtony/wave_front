Which bad endings do random plays reach, and what follows hostility?

One million random plays with a self-preserving AI, each played to the end of the model's horizon (2040 Q4). The bad endings are rare, and one condition gates them all: no play that stays on friendly terms ends in any takeover.

Exploratory, not a registered claim and not a probability. Every player picks at random, so the shares say how often something occurs among random plays, not how likely it is in the world.

  • How the other plays end: 96.0% end with the AI contained (median 2033 Q2), and 3.76% reach 2040 Q4 with no ending. None ends in the model's "coexist" ending, which needs the control balance never to break.
  • An overt takeover is not the end of a play. All 17,633 plays that see one run on to 2040 Q4 with the AI still in control; none is contained afterwards. That is why its share is so much larger than the silent takeover's, which stops the play.
  • Disempowerment and extinction never occur in a million plays. Extinction is not available to a self-preserving AI in this model (only an indifferent one strips human power). Disempowerment needs society locked in with no oversight and no hostile act, which random play does not line up.
  • Why containment dominates: random governments propose treaties and attempt shutdowns constantly. This is the main way random play differs from any realistic play.

The point of no return means governments and labs can no longer guarantee prevention. Against a randomly playing AI, 88% of the plays that reach it still do not end in a takeover. The registered results assume an AI that plays as well as its rules allow, against which the point matters far more.

The points of no return for the other four endings each need one more long computation. They are queued behind the v0.2 kernels and will be added here.

  • Hostility is the gate. Of the 944,224 plays that never turn hostile, none ends in a silent takeover, an overt takeover or a stalemate. In the model a self-preserving AI takes control only once relations are hostile.
  • Once hostile, about 1 play in 100 ends in a silent takeover, and about 1 in 3 sees an overt one.
  • Strong oversight all but removes the silent takeover (2 plays of 48,982), against 15% with no oversight and 3.6% with weak. It does not remove the overt takeover, which still follows in 29% of those plays.
  • Suspicion matters as much. If the AI is not suspected when relations turn hostile, 2.36% end in a silent takeover; if it is already suspected, 0.098%; if its hostility is known, none. A takeover is "silent" only if nobody has noticed by the time the AI holds a majority, so suspicion in time turns it into an overt one.
  • The worst combination is no oversight and no suspicion: 15.93%.

"Ever had strong oversight" and "ever suspected" are true of nearly every 56-quarter random play (99.95% and 98.8%), so the table reads the moment of hostility instead. This reading was added after the first numbers were seen.

How it was done

  • v0.1, self-preserving AI under its profile, from today's state; 1,000,000 plays to the horizon of 56 quarters; every player picks uniformly at random among its available actions; seed 20261117.
  • An overt takeover is a waypoint in the model, counted when a play first shows it. The other bad endings stop the play.
  • The definitions of sections 1 and 2 were committed (be941e6) before any number was computed. Section 3 was added on the owner's next question after the first numbers and is marked so in the script; the same seed drew the same plays.
