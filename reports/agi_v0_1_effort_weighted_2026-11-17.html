When governments and labs take the easy path, the bad endings are 9 to 60 times more common

The owner's proposal: plays that cost less effort should weigh more. Here governments and labs choose each move with a preference for low-effort actions, instead of at random. The AI is self-preserving and still plays at random.

Exploratory, not a registered claim and not a probability. The weighting is a modelling choice (below); a different one would give different shares. One million plays per column, each to 2040 Q4.

3.29%of plays end in a silent takeover (random play: 0.054%)
16.6%reach its point of no return (random play: 0.47%)
15.6%see an overt takeover (random play: 1.76%)
63.5%end with the AI contained (random play: 96.0%)

How the weighting works

Each quarter, governments and labs each pick one of their available actions with probability proportional to the standard normal density at the action's effort score. A zero-effort action is the reference; an action scored 1 is chosen 0.61 times as often, one scored 2 0.14 times, one scored 3 0.011 times. A play's weight therefore falls with every costly move in it. The AI, and chance, still pick uniformly.

Effort scores are the owner's (docs/agi_effort.md): for the labs, deploying, racing and scaling cost 0 and doing nothing costs 3; for governments, doing nothing costs 0 unless there is an alarm, a pause or a shutdown attempt 2 to 3, taking over the labs 3. Reweighting the earlier uniform plays instead would not work: their cheapest cost 14 points, so nearly all the weight would sit on a handful of plays.

  • A treaty proposal stays the most common fix, with the same caution: v0.1 gives treaties no delay.
  • The labs' racing and open-weight releases now appear among the harmful moves. Both cost the labs nothing, so easy-path labs play them often, and both lower oversight.

  • Investing in safety works at 93% of the moments it is available, but it is available less often: it costs the labs effort and is blocked while states race under voluntary rules.

What to keep in mind

  • The weights are a choice. A normal density with unit width was the owner's suggestion. A sharper or flatter preference moves every share here; I have not swept it.
  • The AI is still random. A random self-preserving AI turns hostile and takes control only by chance. An AI that plays well would do better than these shares.
  • These are frequencies among sampled plays, useful for comparing one way of playing with another. They are not forecasts.
