How often do random paths reach the point of no return?

Terms updated on 18 November 2026 to match docs/agi_glossary.html. Numbers and findings are unchanged.

The owner's question: among the paths that cost governments and frontier AI companies the least effort, what share reaches the point of no return early, when the frontier AI system is self-preserving? Answer for random paths: very few. About 1 path in 300 of the cheapest fifth ever reaches the point of no return for a silent takeover, and about 1 in 1,700 reaches it by 2028 Q3. The other 99.66% avoid it for all 24 quarters.

An exploratory statistic, not a pre-registered claim and not a probability of anything. It describes one million paths in which every player picks at random. The model itself has no probabilities.

When paths reach the point of no return

"The zone" is the guarantee zone: the set of states from which governments and frontier AI companies together can still guarantee that no silent takeover happens. A path reaches the point of no return when it leaves that zone. Each line shows the share of paths that have done so by a given quarter.

  • Total effort favours short paths. A path that ends early has fewer actions to pay for. So the cheapest fifth by total effort is mostly paths in which the AI is contained early, and a contained AI cannot take over later. That pulls down its share that reaches the point of no return.
  • Per quarter, the picture reverses slightly. Ranked by effort per quarter, the cheapest fifth reaches the point of no return a little more often than average (0.55% against 0.46%), but later: less often by 2028 Q3.
  • Either way the differences are small. Low effort by governments and frontier AI companies does not make random paths much more or less likely to reach the point of no return.

Why this is not the answer to "how risky is business as usual"

  • Random paths are expensive paths. The cheapest random path cost 14 points and the median 72. The true cheapest paths cost 0 to 2 (claims G29, D2, D3). Random governments keep ordering pauses, proposing treaties and attempting shutdowns, which is why 44% of paths end with the AI contained. The "cheapest fifth" here is far from a world where everyone does the easy thing.
  • The AI picks at random too. A self-preserving AI that picks its actions by chance rarely strings together what a takeover needs. The pre-registered results assume the opposite: an AI that plays as well as its rules allow.
  • The percentage depends on the action lists. Give a player three more irrelevant actions and every share here changes, though nothing that matters has.

What would speak to business as usual: draw paths in which cheap actions are more likely than costly ones ("lazy" players), or let governments and frontier AI companies choose only their zero-effort actions against the AI's best choices. Both can be run on the stored guarantee zone in minutes.

A by-product: the point of no return can come earlier than 2028 Q3

D6 found the point of no return at 2028 Q3 on the shortest path to a takeover. Among the sampled paths, some leave the zone with the round of 2028 Q1 (42 paths in a million), two quarters earlier. So the earliest possible point of no return under self-preserving is 2028 Q1 or sooner. The exact earliest has not been computed.

How it was done

  • Model: v0.1, the frontier AI system self-preserving and true to its goal, from the start state.
  • A path: up to 24 quarters. Each quarter every player (governments, frontier AI companies, the AI, By chance) picks uniformly at random among its available actions (equal-chance sampling). A path stops at an ending.
  • Effort: the action effort of governments and frontier AI companies over the actions chosen (the scores of docs/agi_effort.md).
  • Point of no return: the round after which the path is first outside ⟨⟨H, L⟩⟩□¬SILENT_TAKEOVER. The guarantee zone was computed once (73 minutes, 9.6 GB, 1.5 million memory units) and stored.
  • Sample: 1,000,000 paths, seed 20261117, 7 minutes. Sampling error on the shares of the fifth is about ±0.03 percentage points (ever reaches the point of no return) and ±0.01 (by 2028 Q3).
  • Order of events: the definitions were committed (f660ced) before any number was computed. The ranking by effort per quarter was added after the first numbers were seen and is marked as such; the same seed drew the same paths.
