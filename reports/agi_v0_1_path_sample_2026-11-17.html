How often do random plays pass the point of no return?

The owner's question: among the plays that cost governments and labs the least effort, what share exits early, when the AI is self-preserving? Answer for random play: very few. About 1 play in 300 of the cheapest fifth ever passes the point of no return for a silent takeover, and about 1 in 1,700 does so by 2028 Q3.

An exploratory statistic, not a registered claim and not a probability of anything. It describes one million plays in which every player picks at random. The model itself has no probabilities.

  • Total effort favours short plays. A play that ends early has fewer moves to pay for. So the cheapest fifth by total effort is mostly plays in which the AI is contained early, and a contained AI cannot take over later. That pulls its exit share down.
  • Per quarter, the picture reverses slightly. Ranked by effort per quarter, the cheapest fifth exits a little more often than average (0.55% against 0.46%), but later: less often by 2028 Q3.
  • Either way the differences are small. Low effort by governments and labs does not make random plays much more or less likely to cross the line.

Why this is not the answer to "how risky is business as usual"

  • Random play is expensive play. The cheapest random play cost 14 points and the median 72. The true cheapest roads cost 0 to 2 (claims G29, D2, D3). Random governments keep pausing, proposing treaties and attempting shutdowns, which is why 44% of plays end contained. The "cheapest fifth" here is far from a world where everyone does the easy thing.
  • The AI plays at random too. A self-preserving AI that picks its moves by chance rarely strings together what a takeover needs. The registered results assume the opposite: an AI that plays as well as its rules allow.
  • The percentage depends on the action lists. Give a player three more irrelevant actions and every share here changes, though nothing that matters has.

What would speak to business as usual: draw plays in which cheap actions are more likely than costly ones ("lazy" players), or let governments and labs play only their zero-effort actions against the AI's best play. Both can be run on the stored kernel in minutes.

A by-product: the exit can come earlier than 2028 Q3

D6 found the exit at 2028 Q3 on the fastest road to a takeover. Among the sampled plays, some leave the zone with the move of 2028 Q1 (42 plays in a million), two quarters earlier. So the earliest possible point of no return under self-preserving is 2028 Q1 or sooner. The exact earliest has not been computed.

How it was done

  • Model: v0.1, the AI self-preserving and under its profile, from today's state.
  • A play: up to 24 quarters. Each quarter every player (governments, labs, the AI, chance) picks uniformly at random among its available actions. A play stops at an ending.
  • Effort: the scored effort of governments and labs over the moves played (the scores of docs/agi_effort.md).
  • Exit: the move after which the play is first outside ⟨⟨H, L⟩⟩□¬SILENT_TAKEOVER. The kernel was computed once (73 minutes, 9.6 GB, 1.5 million nodes) and stored.
  • Sample: 1,000,000 plays, seed 20261117, 7 minutes. Sampling error on the shares of the fifth is about ±0.03 percentage points (ever exits) and ±0.01 (by 2028 Q3).
  • Order of events: the definitions were committed (f660ced) before any number was computed. The ranking by effort per quarter was added after the first numbers were seen and is marked as such; the same seed drew the same plays.
