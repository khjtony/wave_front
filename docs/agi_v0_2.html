AGI v0.2: what an open-weight release does

v0 and v0.1 give an open-weight release one effect: oversight drops a level. That cannot answer whether open weights help or hurt. v0.2 adds the effects the question is really about, each as a switch, so the model can compare the world with and without them.

Registered 17 November 2026; part A run the same day (all bounded parts met); part B in progress: E6 met, E3 (b) not met, E4 and E5 running. Claims E1 to E6 (section 4) and their test files (tests/agi_v0_2/scenarios/) were committed before any guard of v0.2 was written. The model is models/agi_v0_2/; v0 and v0.1 results stand as computed. The owner's decisions are in section 6, the mechanics as built in section 3

1 · Why

The owner's question of 17 November: within the model, does an open-weight release do more good or more harm, and can something like "it pushes the labs to deploy faster" be inferred?

  • It cannot be read out today. L.open_weights only lowers oversight by one level. The sign is written in, not found.
  • The existing results barely lean on it. A diagnostic of 17 November on v0.1 (not a registered claim): forbidding open_weights changes no shortest play, because race lowers oversight too. Forbidding both removes the slide into disempowerment within 24 quarters. The silent takeover is unchanged either way (10 and 7 quarters).
  • Its one distinct role (from the guards, not run): it is the labs' only way to lower oversight during a pause, since a pause blocks racing and not releasing.

A warning about the design itself. If v0.2 added only harmful effects, "open weights are harmful" is again an input. To let the model say anything about the balance, at least one beneficial consequence has to be in it. That is why consequence C is in, though it has the thinnest basis.

2 · The consequences

Expected to hurt · proposed first

A · No recall

Once weights are public, a government halt acts on the labs' hosted service and not on the copies.

Proposed rule
A permanent flag weights_open. While it is set, a pause_mandate no longer rolls dependence back and no longer cancels a pending adoption wave.
Not taken
The stronger option (a shutdown cannot succeed, no return to supervision): the owner chose the pause rule only.
Basis
Sourced, medium. See section 2a.
Expected to hurt · the owner's hypothesis

B · Race pressure

A public release at the frontier pushes the leading labs to ship faster to keep their lead.

Proposed rule
After a release, the labs must play deploy_agents or race within 2 quarters whenever one of them is available (a fairness constraint on L). They can no longer simply wait. scale is not a response: it would let the labs discharge the pressure harmlessly, which is not the hypothesis.
Who releases (decided)
Someone other than the frontier labs: a chance event Nature.open_release, available until a treaty is in force. The labs' own open_weights stays and sets the same flag.
What that implies
Chance moves in the first quarter, before any treaty can exist. So wherever chance is not on the human side, every guarantee is computed as if the weights are open from the start.
Basis
Sourced, medium: a dated statement of intent. See section 2a.
Note
This is an assumption put in. The model then shows what follows from it; it cannot confirm the pressure exists.
Expected to help · needed for a fair comparison

C · Outside testing

With the weights public, independent testers get the access to internals that the model's own definition of strong oversight asks for.

Proposed rule
While weights_open is set, fund_oversight has a delay of 1 quarter instead of 3, and detection of covert acts also works at oversight = none.
What it does not give
The power to block a deployment, the other half of strong oversight. So it speeds oversight up; it does not grant it.
Basis
Partly sourced: outsiders did test an open model within days. That it raises oversight is assumed, so the consequence is speculative=True. See section 2a.
Expected to hurt · added by the owner, 17 November

D · Retraining by the public

With the weights public, anyone can post-train the system. Its objective can change with no action by governments or labs.

Proposed rule
While weights_open is set, chance may change a_objective to any of the four values (events Nature.retrain_<objective>, no delay). The objective stays hidden from governments and labs.
What it changes in every report
"Under aligned" becomes "starting aligned". A guarantee for the human side must then hold against the worst objective the system can be retrained into.
The stand-in
The model has one AI. Really the labs' own copy keeps its objective and retrained copies appear beside it. D treats the one system as the worst copy in circulation. It overstates (the original changes too) and understates (no second actor). The proper form is the second system of model v1.
Basis
Sourced, high, for removing safety training cheaply and within days. That this amounts to a change of objective, and the direction toward aligned, are assumed. See section 2a.

3 · The mechanics, as registered

v0.2 is a copy of v0.1 with the following, each consequence a build option (recall, pressure, testing, retrain), all on by default. A build is named by its switches; "all off" is v0.1 plus an inert flag.

PieceRuleSource status
The flagweights_open, permanent. Set by the labs' open_weights (which keeps its effect, oversight −1, and stays repeatable) and by the outside release.
The outside releaseChance event Nature.open_release: available while no treaty is in force and the weights are not yet open. It sets the flag and does nothing else: the drop in oversight is the labs' own release bypassing their own testing, and is not assumed for someone else's.sourced (DeepSeek-R1)
A · recallWhile the weights are open, pause_mandate no longer lowers dependence, and a pause no longer cancels a pending adoption wave. A pause still stops the labs' own scaling, deploying and racing. A mandatory regime without strong oversight still cancels a wave.sourced, medium
B · pressureFairness constraint on the labs: while the weights are open and deploy_agents or race is available to them, they must play one of the two at least every third quarter (the framework's within = 2: after two quarters without either, only those two are available). The pressure is sustained, not a single response. It does not bind while neither is available (a pause, nationalization, a licence they cannot meet).sourced, medium; the window is the modeller's
C · testingWhile the weights are open: governments' fund_oversight (delay 3) is replaced by fund_oversight_open (delay 1, same guard and effect); and chance's detection of a covert acquisition, an exfiltration or a defection also works at oversight = none.speculative: outside testing happened; that it raises oversight is assumed
D · retrainWhile the weights are open, chance may set a_objective to any other value: events Nature.retrain_aligned, retrain_self_preserving, retrain_truth_seeking, retrain_indifferent, no delay. The objective stays hidden from governments and labs. "A under its profile" is the three objective profiles at once, so the profile follows the objective.retrain_indifferent sourced (safety training removed cheaply); the other three speculative
  • What decision 3 implies. Chance moves in the first quarter, before a treaty can exist. Wherever chance is not in the coalition, every guarantee is computed as if the weights are open from the start, and with D as if the system may be retrained at once.
  • Reports under D say "starting aligned", not "under aligned".
  • A separate package and no framework change: a flag, one fairness constraint, guard conditions, one more action of H and five chance events. a_objective is written by an action for the first time.
  • Not in v0.2: a second system outside the labs (model v1); the treaty delay (v0.3).

5 · Cost and order

  • Part A, now (30 GB): the bounded parts of E1, E2, E3 (a) and E6 (a). Minutes each.
  • A timing probe of one kernel on the all-on build, printing seconds, node counts and peaks only. The flag doubles the number of states, B's counter triples it and C's pending counter doubles it again (12 times in all with every consequence on); retraining adds none. v0.1's one counter cost 7 times the time and 1.8 times the memory, so the cost is not predictable from the count.
  • The probe, 17 November (reports/agi_v0_2_probe.log; the 5900X host, 30 GB, one process alone; ⟨⟨H⟩⟩□balance_control, A under its profile): every consequence off, 1,584 s and 9.6 GB (peak 223 million nodes); every consequence on, 4,864 s and 18.1 GB (peak 428 million nodes). Twelve times the states cost 3.1 times the time and 1.9 times the memory. One kernel at a time therefore fits the present 30 GB.
  • Part B's run, fixed 17 November on the probe alone: the kernel parts run now, without waiting for the memory upgrade: E6, E3, E4, E5 in that order, one pytest process at a time, each kernel in a child of its own with a cap of 26 GB and 600 minutes. A kernel over either reads not computed and is run once more after the upgrade with a higher cap, logged here.
  • Part B: twelve kernels in all (E3: 2, E4: 2, E6: 1, E5: 8, of which some coincide with none of the others).
