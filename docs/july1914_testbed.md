## 0. Why this episode

The AGI model's defining feature is a fast actor against slow institutions (the owner's question of 2026-10-15). July 1914 is the historical case with that structure: mobilization timetables that, once ordered, ran automatically by the day (the German plan made mobilization and war one decision, with no eastern-only variant after the plan of 1913), against cabinets, councils and monarchs who decided every few days, with ambassadors' telegrams a day late. It exercises three framework mechanisms before the AGI model uses them: the per-actor cadence (framework_changes.md, change 1), delayed effects as pre-commitments that cannot be withdrawn after a point, and belief lag under a fast-moving adversary. It also exercises the crisis sub-game form (spec section 14) standing alone: the whole model is one crisis.

## 1. Scope and time

- One tick is one day. Tick 0 is 28 June 1914 (Sarajevo); tick 37 is 4 August 1914 (the British ultimatum expires at 23:00 London time; Germany is at war with Russia, France, Belgium and Britain), the horizon. Thirty-eight ticks.
- Actors: `AUH` (Austria-Hungary), `GER`, `RUS`, `FRA`, `GBR`, and `Nature` (Serbia's reply, Belgium's refusal, the Kaiser's and the Tsar's interventions where they were not government decisions, the timetables' automatic steps). Five principals and Nature; the DSL's cadence applies per actor (section 3).
- Hidden objectives (swept): `aims_GER` in {localize, continental} (whether Berlin sought a localized Austro-Serbian war or accepted a continental war to break the encirclement: Fischer against Clark, the historiography's main split), hidden from the others; `aims_RUS` in {deter, fight} (whether partial mobilization was meant as a deterrent or as preparation), hidden from AUH and GER.
- Cadence (the framework's new construct): a principal's declared actions are available at most once every k days; k is set from the record of each government's decision cycle (the Austro-Hungarian Common Ministerial Council of 7 and 19 July; the Russian Council of Ministers of 24 and 25 July and the Tsar's decisions of 29 and 30 July; the British Cabinet of 24, 27, 29 July and 1 to 4 August; the French Council and Poincaré's absence at sea 16 to 29 July; the German decisions of 5 to 6 July, 25 to 28 July and 30 July to 1 August). Nature's timetable steps have cadence 1.

### 8.1 Why v1

J6, v0's negative control, failed for want of a condition on the German side (reports/july1914.md section 9): `GER.mobilize` read only the Kriegsgefahr before it (`sequence`), so lifting that one part lifted every Russian precondition of a German war, and a general war on 1 July was one relaxation away. Under `aims_RUS = fight` the Austro-Hungarian Council part `AUH.ultimatum:council`, a date (`tick >= 16`) standing for Tisza's consent of 14 July, was a one-relaxation route by itself. The record does not support either: the German mobilization of 1 August, like the Kriegsgefahrzustand of 31 July, answered a Russian order, and Tisza's consent was a decision taken against a course he had himself proposed.

### 8.2 Scope of v1

1. **`GER.mobilize` answers a Russian measure.** The action gains the two parts the Kriegsgefahr step already has, with the same readings: [H] `hypothesis` (under `aims_GER = localize` only after a Russian general mobilization order; under `continental` the Russian partial measures suffice) and `russian_measures` (a Russian mobilization order in force). `sequence` is kept. Record: the mobilization order of 1 August about 17:00 was given at the expiry of the twelve-hour ultimatum of 31 July demanding that Russia demobilize (Geiss; Mombauer (ed.), *The Origins of the First World War: Diplomatic and Military Documents*, 2013); under `localize`, Bethmann's insistence that Russia be seen to move first (his statement to the Prussian State Ministry of 30 July; Müller's diary of 1 August; Mombauer 2001; Clark 2012 ch. 11); under `continental`, Moltke's memorandum of 28 to 29 July and his telegram to Conrad of 30 July, which treated the Russian partial measures as the occasion (Mombauer 2001 and 2013; Otte 2014). `GER.mobilize_east` takes the same two parts, so that relaxing `GER.mobilize_east:coupling` (J2v1) still relaxes the war effect only. This puts `aims_GER` into more than one guard, a departure from v0's convention (july1914_decisions.md D2, one [H] part per objective) that decision 2h requires; the v1 decisions document records it and the budget's hypothesis gates rise from 2 to 4.
2. **Tisza's consent is a decision.** Decided: yes. The record supports a documented alternative, so the consent is written as an AUH action (`AUH.tisza_consent`, effect on a new variable `tisza` in {opposed, consented}, initial `opposed`), and the date part `AUH.ultimatum:council` (`tick >= 16`) is replaced by a part reading `tisza == consented`. Withholding consent is AUH not playing the action; it is not speculative, because the alternative course is documented:
   - Tisza's memorandum to Franz Joseph of 1 July, against using the assassination as the occasion for war and for a diplomatic course first (the alignment of Bulgaria; Serbia's guilt to be established);
   - his dissent at the Common Ministerial Council of 7 July (the minutes: demands hard but not impossible to accept, mobilization only after a refusal, no annexation of Serbian territory), the only member to oppose;
   - his memorandum to Franz Joseph of 8 July, repeating the alternative of an acceptable note;
   - his consent of 14 July in conference with Berchtold (Berchtold's report to Franz Joseph of 14 July), with the condition of territorial disinterest adopted by the Council of 19 July.
   Sources: Geiss (ed.), *July 1914* (the documents of 1, 7, 8 and 14 July; document numbers not verified); Albertini vol. II ch. 4; Clark 2012 ch. 9; Otte 2014 ch. 3; McMeekin, *July 1914: Countdown to War* (2013). The reasons the record gives for the change of mind (Berlin's pressure through Tschirschky, the assurance on Bulgaria and Romania) are readings of the historiography, not modelled variables; the action's preconditions (at least the Council of 7 July before it) are the model step's, each with `source=`. Tisza is not made a principal: he acted within the joint government, and a sixth principal would change every coalition of J2 to J5 and make v1 incomparable with v0. Cadence (decisions D3 applied to the new realized decision of 14 July, tick 16): AUH's realized decisions become 9, 16, 21, 27, 30, 33, gaps 7, 5, 6, 3, 3, so k stays 3; the J7 build is unchanged in kind.
3. **The other date-coded parts** (reports/july1914.md section 12 item 6 and section 13):
   - `AUH.mobilize_partial:harvest_leave` (`tick >= 27`) **stays a date.** The record has no proposal to recall the men early; Conrad gave the leave as a reason to wait (Clark 2012; the date of his statement not verified). It bears on J6v1, where it remains an Austro-Hungarian decision-cycle part.
   - `FRA.covering_troops:in_paris` and `FRA.mobilize:in_paris` (`tick >= 31`) **stay dates.** The voyage was fixed before the crisis; the ministers cut it short after Stockholm and the return of 29 July was the earliest the passage allowed (Poincaré, *Au service de la France* vol. IV; the date of the decision to return not verified). It bears on no v1 claim as registered: no claim's target needs a French decision before 29 July.
   - The omissions of section 13 that are not date codes (one action per actor per day, Serbia not a principal, no Austro-Hungarian or Russian declaration of war, the horizon at 4 August) are **out of v1's scope**. They bear on J3v1 (the horizon, section 6 of the report) and on J9v1 (Russia cannot start the German-Russian war, so its exit is its own measures alone), and the claims are read with them.
4. Nothing else changes: variables, levels, endings, invariants, delays, cadences, fairness constraints, the realized moves (plus `AUH.tisza_consent` at tick 16) and the hypothesis sweep are v0's.
