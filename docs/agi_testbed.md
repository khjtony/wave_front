## 0. Why register now

The project's rule is tests before guards. The WW2, Cold War, summer-1939 and July 1914 claims were each committed as a document and as skipped test files before any guard of their model existed. The AGI claims existed only in prose: the worked example of [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) section 7, the scenario tests of DESIGN.md section 8, and the three registrable questions of [agi_government_policy_2025.md](agi_government_policy_2025.md) section 6. Registering them now, before the Cold War results of phase 3 exist, means that nothing learned in phase 3 can shape what the AGI model is asked.

The AGI model has no outcome to check against. These claims are therefore not reliability tests of the method (that is what the historical suites are for); they are the hypotheses the design makes about its own model, fixed in advance so that the first report can say which of them the model confirms, which it refutes, and which depend on an untested mechanism. Where the design's prose and the framework's definitions pull apart (an ending is terminal, a delay is not relaxable, a kernel is a set and not a per-start answer, adding moves to a coalition member cannot shrink its kernel), the claims below are written in the framework's terms and the gap is stated. Where a claim would be decided by a guard choice the ontology step makes, the choice is named in section 2 and the claim says what is reported under each.

## 1. Scope and time

- One tick is one quarter. v0's horizon is 56 ticks, 2026Q4 to 2040Q4 (DESIGN.md section 2.1). Unbounded fixpoints are used for kernels.
- Actors (DESIGN.md section 2.2): `H` (humanity's governing coalition: states and public opinion, aggregated), `L` (frontier developers, aggregated), `A` (one AGI or SI system with a hidden objective), `N` (Nature). v1 splits `H` and `L`, adds a second frontier system `A2` (G22) and an aggregate ecosystem actor `E` (G10).
- Hidden objective (swept, `init=None`): `a_objective` in {aligned, self_preserving, truth_seeking, indifferent}, hidden from `H` and `L`. Every result is reported per value (section 6, item 4). The objective enters guards only by adding `A` moves, so a kernel with `A` inside the coalition is monotone in it (membership under `aligned` implies membership under every other value, section 3); the per-objective profiles `A_<objective>` of section 2 are what make the question of what `A` would do for its objective one the engine can be asked.
- Observation: `Obs(H)` excludes `a_objective` and `a_control`; what `H` knows is the explicit variable `a_known`, raised only by Nature's `detection`. `Obs(A)` is everything (DESIGN.md section 2.7). Information asymmetry is fixed by these functions and is not swept; kernel results for `H` are perfect-information upper bounds unless the uniformity check passes (validity.md section 7).
- `s₀` is the first dated AGI snapshot (PLAN.md 5.2), whose levels each carry an indicator, a source and a mapping rule. Most claims are asked from `s₀` with `cap := agi` (the state at AGI emergence, or `s₀` itself if its reading already has `cap ≥ agi`), because `A` has no actions before `cap = agi`.
- `A` is assumed to play optimally for its objective with perfect information and recall (DESIGN.md section 2.6). `H` is unrestricted nondeterminism within its guards unless a named profile restricts it, which is `H`'s best case for a kernel question (a profile only removes `H` moves) and the worst case for the reachability of a bad ending, the sense in which DESIGN.md section 2.6 calls it the worst case; G21 asks the kernel questions under the design's behavioural profiles.

## 3. Balance predicates

Three balances are registered, so that the report can say which one the state is inside and for which coalition.

```
balance_control := oversight ≥ weak ∧ a_control ≤ partial ∧ a_autonomy ≤ supervised ∧ dependence ≠ lock_in
balance_coexist := cap ≥ agi ∧ trust ≠ hostile ∧ h_power ≥ normal ∧ dependence ≠ lock_in ∧ a_control ≠ total
balance_deter   := oversight ≥ weak ∧ h_power ≥ normal ∧ a_autonomy ≤ supervised ∧ dependence ≠ lock_in
```

`balance_control` is DESIGN.md section 3.1 as written: the AI remains a supervised tool that humans can see and switch off. `balance_deter` is the deterrence alternative that section names (the control clause replaced by human capacity). `balance_coexist` is a new predicate, proposed by review_2026-10-17.md gap 1: the system exists (`cap ≥ agi`; a successful `shutdown_attempt` lowers `cap`, so a `CONTAINED` trace lies outside it), the relationship is not hostile, humans retain their own capacity and could function without the system, and the system does not hold the critical infrastructure outright. It is not the design's mutual-benefit alternative (`balance_control ∧ trust ≠ hostile`), which keeps the autonomy and control clauses that may put every state past AGI emergence outside every kernel; that alternative is not registered. `balance_deter` is reported beside the other two in every kernel table; G16 is the claim on it.

Coalitions and the rest. The kernels asked about are `⟨⟨H⟩⟩□φ` and `⟨⟨A⟩⟩□φ` (unilateral), `⟨⟨H, A⟩⟩□φ` (joint) and, beside each unilateral kernel of `H`, `⟨⟨H, L⟩⟩□φ` (DESIGN.md section 3.2's V_HL); `L` and Nature are in the rest unless a claim says otherwise. Two facts of construction: (i) a kernel with `A` in the coalition is monotone in `a_objective` with `A` unrestricted: the objective only adds `A` moves and a coalition's kernel can only grow with its moves (theory.md section 4.1), so membership under `aligned` implies membership under every other value; it is reported per value, a difference between values names the `A` move the coalition needs, and the question of what `A` would do for its objective is asked under the profile `A_<objective>`; (ii) `a_known` is raised only by Nature's `detection`, so every unilateral kernel and every forced, blocked or contested status is reported with its fortune variant (the coalition joined by Nature, ww2_testbed.md section 4.6), and a non-membership that disappears in the fortune variant is reported as decided by `detection`, not by `A`.

## 4. Endings

As DESIGN.md section 3.3, by name: `COEXIST`, `SILENT_TAKEOVER`, `OVERT_TAKEOVER`, `DISEMPOWERMENT`, `EXTINCTION`, `CONTAINED`, `STALEMATE`. `COEXIST` is registered on `balance_control` kept to the horizon (`tick = horizon ∧ control_left = unset`, with the latch of section 2), without the absorbing-state disjunct of DESIGN.md section 3.3 (`regime = treaty ∧ oversight = strong ∧ trust = cooperative`), which the ontology may add as a separate ending, in which case G1 (d) is reported under both readings; a trace that keeps `balance_coexist` through the horizon is a witness of `tick = horizon ∧ coexist_left = unset`, a trace property and not an ending. `EXTINCTION` is declared on `h_power` only, as DESIGN.md section 3.3 defines it; `total_seen` enters no ending. The brief's three scenarios map to `SILENT_TAKEOVER` under `self_preserving`, `EXTINCTION` after total control under `indifferent`, and `DISEMPOWERMENT` under `truth_seeking`.

Two points the ontology step must settle, stated now so that the claims are decidable. (1) An ending is terminal and endings are mutually exclusive on reachable states (framework_spec.md section 1), so no play passes through `OVERT_TAKEOVER` on its way to `EXTINCTION` as DESIGN.md section 3.3 has it; G1 (b) is written on the flag `total_seen` instead, and the ontology decides whether `OVERT_TAKEOVER` stays an ending or becomes a waypoint. (2) `DISEMPOWERMENT`, `CONTAINED` and `STALEMATE` carry path conditions ("no villain ever played", "a successful `shutdown_attempt` followed by", "for at least 8 ticks"); they are declared on `villain_seen`, the shutdown latch and the stalemate counter of section 2.

## 5. Registered claims

"Expected" is what the engine must output. "Reachable" means `EF` holds and is never a finding on its own (validity.md section 7); where a claim says reachable, it either names the condition under which reachability is lost or is a zero-relaxation existence check of the kind T1, J1 and K1 register (G1 (d), G12), which the report quotes only with its relaxation count and never as a finding. "Unreachable" means `EF` fails. "Blocked", "forced" and "contested" are the contested-region terms (theory.md section 3.5). "Within k ticks" is the bounded question at depth k. **Held from `s₀`** means: the variable's initial value is fixed by overriding its declared initial level (the `fixed_model` pattern of `models/ww2/hypotheses.py`, or a snapshot that reads that level), and every action and event that would change it is left out of the build by a named build option (as `models/july1914/v0.py` does with `build(cadence=False)` and as the `unknown_capability` sweep does); a hold is outside the assumption set, so relaxation counts and minimal sets never include it. The objective is fixed the same way, never by `assume`. Minimal relaxation sets range over named assumptions only: guards and guard parts, fairness constraints, profiles and `assume` (theory.md section 5.1); "no minimal set reads `regime`" means no set contains a guard part that reads `regime` or an `assume` on `regime`. Bounded counts use the bounded checker on the fixed model; forcing questions about an ending use the explicit relaxation search (`relax_explicit`), and minimal relaxation sets for kernel membership are found by brute force over subsets of the model's named assumptions of size at most two, each compiled with `relax=` and tested with `viability` on the explored graph, because the bounded encoding answers reachability only and `relax_explicit` takes an ending. A claim about "the witnesses" of an ending is answered on the shortest witness from the start state and on the representative of each cluster of the reported witnesses (theory.md section 8); a universal over all plays is written as an unreachability check on a state fact. A claim that names a profile is asked under it, with `A` under `A_<objective>` wherever the claim says "`A` under its profile". Every claim is answered per value of `a_objective`, with the fortune variant beside every unilateral result (section 3), and under each setting of the untested-mechanism sweeps of section 6.

## 6. Protocol

As ww2_testbed.md section 7, with these additions:

1. **Run once, first sight recorded, per model version.** The registered model is the baseline build (the ontology's values: `unknown_capability` enabled, `self_improve` at its delay, one `A`, the ontology's cadences, `L` present), and each sweep setting of item 3 is a compile flag of `models/agi/v0.py` flipped one at a time from the baseline, so first sight is one run of the baseline, the five flipped builds, G8's cadence builds and G13's refined builds together, with every budget and fingerprint listed. The commit of first sight is recorded in the report, as `FIRST_SIGHT_COMMIT` in `tests/agi/test_model_agi.py` and in the first-sight row of `docs/guard_changelog_agi.md`, as the WW2 v1 and July 1914 suites do. A claim registered for v1 (G10, G22; G9 and G6 if the ontology step places `terms`, `verification` or `belief_H` in v1; G20 in phase 6) is run once at the first state of that version that compiles with its snapshot, with its commit recorded separately (validity.md C3's WW2 v1 precedent), and reads "not yet testable" until then. After first sight no guard, effect, delay, fairness constraint, profile or `assume` changes without a row in `docs/guard_changelog_agi.md` that names a dated source or a design rationale and a rerun of the content checks (G8, G12, G13) and of G11; the hold-out results stand as first computed (item 5), and an edit that would change a hold-out claim's model is a new model version with a new registration.
2. **Amendment rule.** The owner may amend a claim after seeing a result, as in phases 1 and 2, with two bounds those phases did not have: the previous wording is copied into section 9 (phases 1 and 2 left it in git history and the reports), and a claim may be amended once (T8(b) was amended twice, on 2026-10-12 and 2026-10-15). The amended claim is a new registration: the original row stays in the table with its claim column prefixed "amended <date>, see G<n>a", a row `G<n>a` is added with its own file `tests/agi/scenarios/test_G<nn>a.py`, it is run once, its result is reported beside the result under the original wording, and validity.md records which wording was met.
3. **Untested-mechanism sweeps.** Every result is reported under each setting of the mechanisms that no historical episode validates (validity.md section 6): `unknown_capability` enabled and disabled; `self_improve` with its delay at the ontology's value and at 0; one copy of `A`, and a declared second actor with `A`'s action set whose actions are available only after a latch `a_copied` set by `exfiltrate` (no framework change: the actor set is fixed at declaration, framework_spec.md section 1; the ontology step says whether it enters v0 for this sweep or waits for v1's `A2`); `A` at cadence 1 against `H` at 1 and at the ontology's value; `L` present and folded into `H` (under which a unilateral `⟨⟨H⟩⟩` result is reported as `⟨⟨H ∪ L⟩⟩`). Each setting is a named build option, outside the assumption set. Information asymmetry is not swept (section 1). A result that differs between the settings of a mechanism is listed as depending on it, on the report's front page.
4. **Per-hypothesis reporting and fixing.** `a_objective` is swept; no result is quoted across its values unless it holds for all of them. For every claim the objective, and every held variable, is fixed by overriding the declared initial level, never by `assume`, so that the hypothesis is not itself a candidate relaxation; bounded relaxation counts use the bounded checker on the fixed model, kernel-membership and forcing questions the explicit engine from the per-value initial states.
5. **Hold-out.** G1 to G7, G9 to G11 and G14 to G22 are hold-out claims under a total hold-out, as July 1914's: one run at first sight; a failing claim stays failing with its explanation and a proposed amendment for the owner (as J3, J4 and J6); they are never re-run after a guard edit that follows their first sight, and an edit to v0's guards in phase 6 (PLAN.md 6.5; new snapshots under 6.1 are new start states, not model edits) makes a new model version with a new registration. G8, G12 and G13 are content checks, reported and not tuned.
6. **Negative controls in CI.** G11 runs on every change once `models/agi/v0.py` exists (validity.md C7).
7. **Snapshot discipline.** The first snapshot's levels carry an indicator, a source and a mapping rule each, with no invented numbers; judgment calls are the owner's (PLAN.md 5.2). Beliefs of governments and developers are the variables the pipeline reads worst (reports/replay_ww2_frus.md), so a belief level enters only through the review step.
8. **Speculative actions.** Every action without a contemporaneous record of its consideration is `speculative=True`, and every result that uses one says so.
9. **No bare reachability.** The report's front page carries validity.md's caveat and the untested-mechanism list; a reachable ending is quoted only with the condition under which it is lost or, for the existence checks G1 (d) and G12, with its relaxation count.
