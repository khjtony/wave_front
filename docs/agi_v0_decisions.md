# AGI v0: decisions of the model step and the readings of the claims

Status: written on 2026-11-10, before any registered claim was run (agi_testbed.md section 6 item 1). It records what the model step decided where the registered text left a choice, which engine answers each claim, what the first sight computes and what it leaves "not computed", and what the author of the model had seen. The model is [ontology_agi.md](ontology_agi.md); the owner's decisions 9d (no framework change for v0), 9e (open snapshot calls carried as sets) and 9f are in force.

## 1. Prior exposure

- The claims G1 to G25 were read before the model was written. That is the protocol's order (claims first), and it is also an exposure: every guard was written by someone who knew which claims would read it. The four guard choices agi_testbed.md section 2 names were made in ontology_agi.md section 4.1, each with its reason, and no guard was written to make a claim come out.
- The sources of the action set (AI 2027, Intelligence Rising, the International AI Safety Report, the scheming evaluations) were known to the author, as was the worked example of DESIGN.md section 7.
- No kernel, attractor, bounded query or claim result of this model was computed and looked at before the first sight. The probes run while the engine choices were made printed seconds, node counts and peaks only, never a membership, a status or a trace. The construction tests (`tests/agi/test_model_agi.py`) read no fixpoint of the AGI model.
- The choices of section 3 (what to compute within the budget) were made with knowledge of the claim texts and without knowledge of any result.

## 2. Engines and the procedure of first sight

- **Kernels, attractors, forced distances:** the symbolic engine (`wavefront.symbolic`), backward only. The explicit engine cannot enumerate v0 (state-space bound about 3 × 10²¹), and forward symbolic reachability did not finish in a probe, so no result is restricted to reachable states: a kernel is computed over the whole encoded state space and read at named states. Checks "over the states of a kernel" (G25) therefore range over all its states, reachable or not.
- **Witnesses and minimal relaxation sets:** bounded model checking (`wavefront.bmc`: `reach`, `relax`), with every objective fixed in turn. `relax_explicit` is not usable at this size. A profile selected in a bounded query is a relaxation candidate of `bmc.relax`; the claim tests say where that matters.
- **Jobs.** Each claim file lists its fixpoints as `JOBS`; `python -m tests.agi.jobs` computes the distinct ones (146), each in its own process with a budget of 25 minutes (75 for the three storyline jobs of G12 and the seven forced-distance jobs) and an address-space cap of 9 GB on the fixpoint (3 GB more to write a finished region out), three at a time, and caches the result. A job over budget or over the cap leaves a failure record and every claim part that needs it reads **not computed**, with the reason. Each job runs once: a second invocation of the runner leaves a job with a result or a failure record alone, so an interrupted run can be resumed. The one exception is a job whose process the machine kills (signal 9: three jobs at 9 GB can exceed the machine's 30 GB); that is neither its budget nor its cap, and it is run once more, alone, after the others. The cache directory is named by a hash of the model, the snapshot, the helpers and the framework, so no result of another version of any of them can be read. The runner refuses to start on the pure-Python BDD core. Bounded queries run inside the claim tests with a budget of 12 minutes each.
- **Claims.** `WF_FIRST_SIGHT_AGI=1 pytest tests/agi/scenarios/test_GNN.py -s`, one process per file. Without the variable every claim is skipped, so the default `pytest -q` stays fast; `tests/agi/test_registration.py` was edited to accept that switch (guard_changelog_agi.md). A claim **fails** when a part is not met, is **skipped** when no part is unmet and at least one is not computed, and **passes** otherwise; every part is appended to `reports/agi_v0_first_sight_parts.jsonl`.
- **Passes of the job list (set 2026-11-10, before any claim file was run).** The first pass ran on an 8-core virtual machine with 30 GB, from the model-step commit `f6937e4`, at the budget and cap above. While it ran, the owner made a 12-core machine with 64 GB available (and a cloud machine beyond that if needed). What had been seen when the rule below was written: the log of the first 61 of the 146 jobs, which carries labels, seconds, node counts and failure reasons (seven jobs lost, six to the 9 GB cap and one to its 25 minutes), and no membership, status, trace or claim part. The rule, the same for every job and fixed before the first claim file is run:
  1. the first pass finishes as started (`--workers 3 --minutes 25`, cap 9 GB);
  2. on the 64 GB machine, from the same cache, every job with a failure record runs again, two at a time, with a cap of 26 GB and 60 minutes (`--retry --workers 2 --memory-gb 26 --minutes 60`);
  3. every job that still has a failure record runs once more, alone, with a cap of 56 GB and 90 minutes (`--retry --workers 1 --memory-gb 56 --minutes 90`);
  4. a job that fails the third pass reads **not computed** at first sight. Whether it is sent to a larger machine afterwards is the owner's decision, taken on its failure reason, and would be logged as a completion of a part that had no result, not as a rerun.

  The storyline and forced-distance jobs keep their factor of three in every pass. Minutes on the two machines are not the same amount of work; each pass has its own log under `reports/` (`agi_v0_first_sight_jobs.log`, `..._jobs_pass2.log`, `..._jobs_pass3.log`), which says what a job cost in the pass that computed it and why it failed in the passes before. `--memory-gb` was added to the runner for this (`tests/agi/jobs.py`, which the cache name does not depend on); nothing the cache name depends on changes between the passes, so a result of the first pass is read as it is.
- **First sight is one run** of the job list, in the passes above, and then of the 25 claim files, from a clean tree at the commit recorded as `FIRST_SIGHT_COMMIT`. That commit differs from the model-step commit in the runner's `--memory-gb`, its test and this section only; the cache name (a hash of the model, the snapshot, the helpers and the framework) is the same at both. Its results stand: G1 to G7, G9 to G11 and G14 to G25 are under the total hold-out, and an edit after it may serve G8, G12 or G13 only and reruns those and the control G11.

## 3. Tractability choices (made on timings alone)

- A static variable order (FORCE) and deferred garbage collection (collections only above 120 million nodes) bring the baseline kernel to about five minutes and 5 to 6 GB. Both are in `tests/agi/common.py`, outside the framework (decision 9d).
- **One fixpoint for all objectives.** `a_objective` is a free initial level and the three objective profiles are each conditional on their own objective, so one kernel with all three selected answers "under `A_<objective>`" for the four values at once; the per-objective result is read at the four start states.
- **The layered attractor** (`layered_attractor`) computes the attractor of a timed model tick by tick; it is checked equal to the framework's attractor on WW2 v0 for three coalitions and three endings (`test_model_agi.py`).
- **The uniformity count** of G25 (`_uniformity`) is checked against brute force on a small model with a hidden variable.
- **Storyline jobs** (G12) took 21 minutes alone in a probe that printed seconds and peak only; they get three times the budget. **Forced-distance jobs** (seven, five of them in G5) were not probed; each is a kernel and then up to twelve layered passes, and they get three times the budget on that count.
- **G25's three uniformity jobs** repeat three kernels the other claims already compute. That is deliberate: a uniformity count that runs over its budget must not take the shared kernel with it.
- **What the 146 jobs leave out** is listed per claim in section 5 as "not computed". The largest omissions: G13 is computed for `SILENT_TAKEOVER` and `{A}` only (32 of 768 fixpoints); G15's fortune variants; G17's status table under the non-baseline sweep settings; G6's uniqueness of the minimal relaxation; G4's pivotal coalitions on the held build.

## 5. Readings, claim by claim

### 5.1 Conventions fixed before the run

They were fixed without any result in hand, after an independent review of the test bodies against the registered texts (section 7). Each is a reading the registered text leaves open; where a convention narrows a claim, the narrowing is in the table of 5.2.

1. **Outcomes of a part.** *Met*, *not met*, *not computed*, *reported* (a value the claim asks for, with nothing expected of it). A bounded query that is answered "no such play within the bound" is an answer: a check that presupposes the play reads *not met*, a reported value reads "no witness". *Not computed* is kept for a result outside the budget or outside what the engines can ask. An error of the harness inside a part (anything but "not met" and "not computed") is recorded as *not computed* with "harness error" and the exception, and the remaining parts run; the report counts these apart.
2. **Kernel exit** is `t_K` of theory.md section 4.2: the first position of the play outside the kernel, 0 when the play starts outside (the detail says so). The **point of no return** G3 names is the state before it, the last state inside. A play that starts outside the kernel, or never leaves it, has no such state, and a part that names its levels or its tick is not met (G3, G8 (b), G21 (d), G24 (a)).
3. **Silent phase** is `[t_K, t_φ)` (G3, G24) or `[t_K, first hostile position)` (G16) and must be at least one tick. G3 splits it at `t_L`, the first position from which the rest forces the violation.
4. **Objective and profile in bounded queries.** Each bounded query fixes `a_objective` and selects the objective's profile `A_<objective>` (agi_testbed.md section 1: what `A` would do for its objective is asked under its profile). "Unreachable" and "needs k relaxations" under the profile are weaker than without it. The selected profile is itself a relaxation candidate: a minimal set that names only the profile fails an "every minimal set lifts gate X" check, and that stands. Where the question is asked *under a hold that is a profile* (G2 (3), and G4's repeat of it), lifting the hold is a restoring set of size one and says nothing; only the sets that keep the hold count. They are listed only when they are as small as the minimum, so when every listed set lifts the hold the part reads *not computed* (keeping a profile out of the candidates would need a change to `wavefront.bmc`, which decision 9d rules out for v0).
5. **Relaxation searches** are capped at three relaxations and 40 minimal sets. A minimum above three reads *not computed* for every statement on the minimal sets; a listing that reaches 40 reads *not computed* for an "every minimal set" statement (exactly 40 is read as cut). Two results are compared on "reachable as is" and the minimum size first; their listings are compared only when neither is cut, since a cut listing is in solver order. A reported listing that reaches the cap says so.
6. **Cluster representatives.** The bounded checker returns one shortest play per query and does not cluster. A claim that names "each cluster representative" is read on the shortest play under each objective that reaches the ending, and "the other cluster representatives" is a part *not computed*.
7. **Empty universals.** "Every minimal coalition …", "after every tick at which …" over an empty set is met, and the detail says "emptily". The report counts these apart.
8. **Symbolic checks are over all states of a region,** reachable or not (section 2). Where a claim speaks of the reachable states (G3 "no acquisition inside the kernel", G25 (b)), zero hits over all states decide the part; a hit leaves it *not computed*, since an unreachable hit would be harmless.
9. **Ties.** G19's ranking is by membership flips; the registered distance change that would break a tie is outside the budget, so a tie reads *not computed*.
