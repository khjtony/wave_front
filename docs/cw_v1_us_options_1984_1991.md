# American crisis-initiating options, 1984 to 1991: a source search before cw_v1's first sight

Status: research note, 2026-10-27, written on the owner's request before the first run of V1 to V10 (coldwar_testbed.md section 9). It changes no model, guard or claim. It answers the open point of [cw_v1_decisions.md](cw_v1_decisions.md) D2 and its last section ("The American options after 1983"): whether any documented, argued American option of 1984 to 1991 would have opened or escalated a crisis with the USSR.

Marks: **[V]** verified against the source online on 2026-10-27; **[S]** standard secondary citation, not checked against the document; **[U]** uncertain.

## 1. The test

An option qualifies for `models/cw/v1.py` beside `confront_cienfuegos`, `confront_brigade` and `blockade_cuba` only if it passes all three of the following:

1. **Rule 1 of [actions_cw.md](actions_cw.md) section 1.** The option is a decision taken or debated at the time. A dated record of its consideration exists, and the alternative taken is documented.
2. **The effect `esc +1` from peace.** The option would plausibly have produced the `crisis` rung of [ontology_cw.md](ontology_cw.md): "a standing confrontation between the principals carried across a 1 January (an ultimatum with a deadline, forces deployed against each other)". An incident resolved within days or weeks does not meet this, and neither does a diplomatic quarrel or a move in an arms race.
3. **Consistency with the record.** If the option was *taken*, the 1 January record after it must show the crisis. Every 1 January from 1984 to 1991 has `esc = peace` (ontology section 5). So a taken option coded `esc +1` would contradict the realized path. Only a rejected or deferred option can carry the effect.

## 2. Result

**No candidate passes all three tests.** The search found no documented, argued and rejected American option of 1984 to 1991 whose adoption would plausibly have produced a standing US–Soviet confrontation across a 1 January. Some candidates were debated, but their effect falls on other variables (`arms`, `afghan`) or on no variable. Others were taken, and the record shows no crisis followed. The rest have no contemporaneous record of the option at all.

The FRUS volumes on the Soviet Union for these years were searched in full text: FRUS 1981–88 vol. IV (Jan 1983–Mar 1985), vol. V (Mar 1985–Oct 1986) and vol. VI (Oct 1986–Jan 1989), using the TEI files of HistoryAtState/frus. They contain no option paper for a military confrontation with the USSR in these years. Volume VI does not mention the Black Sea at all.

V2 should therefore record this as a finding: **from 1984 the American side has no crisis-opening move in the catalogue, and the search did not find one in the record.** V2's non-membership of `⟨⟨USSR⟩⟩□balance_core` for 1984 to 1991 must come from what the model already has, or fail. It must not come from options added to make it pass.

### 3.1 Notes on each

1. **Black Sea, 13 March 1986.** The two ships stayed in Soviet waters for about two hours. Moscow put its Black Sea air and naval forces on combat readiness. The protest note warned of "serious consequences", and Chernavin and Arbatov said the ships might be fired on next time (Aceves 1993, pp. 65–66, citing UPI 22 Mar 1986 and the Soviet note). The transit was approved inside the executive branch; such operations need JCS or presidential approval (Aceves p. 62). The alternative of exempting the USSR from the Freedom of Navigation programme is on record as rejected. But the option was *taken*, and the 1 January 1987 record is peace: the episode ended with a protest. It fails test 3.
2. **Black Sea, 12 February 1988.** This is the strongest naval candidate. The Soviet ships announced beforehand that they had been "authorized to strike". The CIA judged the bumping was "decided at the highest political level". Carlucci answered the argument for not transiting: "You can always find reasons for not doing it at some particular point in time" (SASC testimony, quoted in Aceves p. 69). This is the alternative argued and rejected. But again the option was taken. It led to bilateral consultations, the Crowe–Akhromeyev statement on dangerous military activities (11 July 1988), and the Uniform Interpretation on innocent passage signed by Baker and Shevardnadze at Jackson Hole on 23 September 1989 (Aceves pp. 71–73). It de-escalated, and fails test 3. No later transit, and no escalated variant (an escorted repeat, or a transit against an explicit threat to fire), was found as a debated option.
3. **Forward exercises.** These were taken policy and were not debated as crisis-opening alternatives. The Soviet answer was exercises of its own (FRUS v04 d221 lists the Norwegian Sea exercise of 1984 among Soviet activity). No 1 January shows a crisis. FleetEx 83-1 and Able Archer fall before 1984; Able Archer is already a sub-game.
4. **Nicholson.** Shultz's memorandum of 30 March 1985 records only a diplomatic course: a meeting of the commanders-in-chief, Otis and Zaytsev, which the Soviets accepted. The press reported that officials excluded "serious economic or political sanctions". No harsher option is on record, so there is nothing to catalogue.
5. **Daniloff.** The memorandum of 3 September 1986 records "strong sentiment ... for taking punitive steps". It warns that each option risked "a PNG war" (mutual expulsions), and that "more dramatic steps" would follow if the proposals failed. The steps were expulsions: 25 of the Soviet UN Mission, then 55 more in October. No military measure appears. The affair was settled on 30 September 1986, inside the year. It fails test 2.
6. **Stingers.** This was a real debate. The JCS and the CIA were at first reluctant over the Soviet reaction, and the alternative of not supplying the missiles is documented. Matlock wrote to Poindexter on 2 April 1986 that "such moves as supplying stingers to the mujahedin can be most useful". The option was taken, and its effect is on the Afghan war (`afghan`) and Soviet costs. There was no standing US–Soviet confrontation; Shevardnadze raised it only as a matter for restraint (v06 d44). It fails tests 2 and 3. The exact decision date is [S]: early 1986 in Kuperman, while a CIA-held press item of 7 May 1986 says "earlier this month".
7. **The broad interpretation.** This was debated and rejected, and the record is good. Ikle and Perle argued at the Senior Arms Control Policy Group on 4 October 1985 for a programme beyond the broad reading. Nitze and Shultz carried the NSPG, and NSDD 192 of 11 October 1985 kept the programme within the traditional reading while asserting that the broad reading was legal (FRUS v44 pt. 1 d78). The debate came back in February 1987 (FRUS v06 has 8 mentions). But the effect of the option is on the treaty regime (`arms`, `buildup`), not a standing confrontation of forces. The Soviet answer threatened at the time was countermeasures and linkage in Geneva, not an ultimatum. It fails test 2. If the owner wants it in the model, it belongs as an `arms` option, which is a different claim from V2.
8. **The MiG red line, November 1984.** Hartman's démarche, and Bessmertnykh's reply that "our fears were groundless", are contemporaneous (v04 d303, 7 Nov 1984). Shultz's "we would take them out" is in a memoir of 1993, quoted in the FRUS footnote. The cargo turned out to be helicopters. In this episode the crisis-opening move would have been *Soviet* (delivering the MiGs), with an American strike as the response. So it is not an American option against Soviet restraint, which is what V2's control removes. If anything, it would be a Soviet option, and it is not needed for V2.
9. **Strikes on Nicaragua, and a quarantine.** Gates's memorandum to Casey of 14 December 1984 urged air strikes on Nicaragua's buildup. It was not adopted [S]. Its target is Nicaragua, not Soviet forces, and the precedent of Grenada (October 1983, with Soviet personnel present) produced only protests. The mining of Nicaraguan harbours, which damaged the Soviet tanker *Lugansk* on 20 March 1984, was taken and answered by a protest. That is evidence against an `esc` effect. Unlike Haig's Cuban blockade of 1981, no record was found of a quarantine of Nicaragua argued at the NSC or NSPG and rejected; there are only press reports and a CIA analytic memorandum [U]. It fails tests 1 and 2.
10. **Vilnius, January 1991.** The options reported were rhetorical condemnation, suspending technical exchanges, aid, and the date of the Moscow summit. Policy was bounded by the need for Soviet support in the Gulf. No military option is on record, so it fails test 2.
11. **The August coup.** Bush's first statement was guarded. Nothing found records a military or alert option, and the coup collapsed within three days. It fails tests 1 and 2.
12. **Libya and SALT II.** Both were taken. Moscow cancelled a foreign ministers' meeting after the Libya raid, and listed both moves as grievances (Matlock's mock memorandum, v05 d240). No crisis followed. The SALT II decision bears on `arms`. Both fail test 3.

## 4. Recommendation

- **Add no American option for 1984 to 1991.** None meets rule 1 together with an `esc` effect consistent with the record. Coding a taken transit as `esc +1` would contradict the realized path (test 3). Coding the broad interpretation or the Stingers as crisis options would assign them an effect the record does not support.
- **Record the absence as a finding in cw_v1_decisions.md D2.** The present wording "none documented as argued and rejected was found" can stand. This note should be cited as the search behind it. When V2 is run, its report should say that from 1984 the United States' only ways out of `balance_core` are those the model already has: `USA.escalate` needs a standing crisis, and in these years only Nature or a Soviet move can create one. The years 1984 to 1991 are therefore expected to be inside `⟨⟨USSR⟩⟩□balance_core` unless such a route exists. The report should state this as a property of the record, not hide it.
- **If the owner nevertheless wants one candidate considered**, the least weak is the Black Sea transit of February 1988. Its alternative is documented (Carlucci's testimony) and so is Soviet authorization to use force. The honest coding would be a Nature risk of an incident, not `esc +1`. That is a new mechanism and would be a change to V2 made before first sight; it is the owner's call, not this note's.
