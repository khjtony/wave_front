# July 1914 v0: decisions for the model and the readings of J1 to J7

Inputs: the registered claims ([july1914_testbed.md](july1914_testbed.md), committed in 7e533d4 before any guard), the architect's decisions for the model step (recorded here as D1 to D9, with what the historian settled under each), the protocol of [ww2_testbed.md](ww2_testbed.md) section 7 and the "contested" reading of its section 4.6, the framework's cadence ([framework_changes.md](framework_changes.md), change 1; [framework_spec.md](framework_spec.md) 3.1 and 3.1a), and the WW2 conventions ([ww2_v1_decisions.md](ww2_v1_decisions.md)). Content: [ontology_1914.md](ontology_1914.md), [actions_1914.md](actions_1914.md), `data/july1914/`. Model: `models/july1914/v0.py`. This document was written, with the readings of section R, before any claim was run. Nothing here edits a registered claim.

## D1. Time

- One tick is one day; tick 0 is 28 June 1914. **The state at tick t is the start of day t**; the move at tick t is what was decided on day t (after midnight counts on the day); effects are visible at t + 1. A reaction to another actor's decision of the same day appears the next day ([SQR]). Nature plays one event per tick ([NSL] where two fall together).
- The architect's "tick 37 is 4 August 1914, the horizon" is read as: tick 37 is the last day of decisions, and the DSL horizon is 38, the state after 4 August. With a DSL horizon of 37, the state at the start of 4 August would be terminal, and the British ultimatum and the entry into Belgium, which the testbed dates to 4 August, could not be played. Settled by the historian; the report states it.
- Windows in the claims are read on this clock: "departure inside ticks 31 to 34" is the first state of the path, at the start of a day, outside the kernel; "within two days" from a decision at tick t is a state at tick t + 2 at the latest.

## D2. Actors and Nature

`AUH`, `GER`, `RUS`, `FRA`, `GBR` and `Nature`. Nature carries Serbia's reply, the arrival in Berlin of Lichnowsky's telegrams of 29 July and 1 August (the belief events), and the timetable's entry into Belgium. The Kaiser's Halt in Belgrade is played by GER (`restrain_vienna`) because Bethmann transmitted it (a government act); the Tsar's countermand of 29 July is RUS's `revoke` (the architect: "revoke exists for RUS"). Belgium's refusal has no separate event (ontology section 4). Hidden objectives: `aims_GER` in {localize, continental} hidden from the other principals; `aims_RUS` in {deter, fight} hidden from AUH and GER; no `assume`; the report sweeps the four pairs. Each enters exactly one guard as an [H] part (actions catalogue).

## D3. Cadence

The architect: cadence from the record of each government's decision cycle, set before any claim is run; where a government decided on consecutive days the cadence must allow it; "choose the smallest k that the record's decision dates satisfy". Read as: **k is the smallest gap, in days, between two consecutive realized declared decisions of that government** (the realized declared decisions are those in `data/july1914/decisions.csv`; any k up to that gap lets every realized decision stand, and the gap is the tightest refractory period the record supports). Days on which a body met and decided nothing the model represents (the British Cabinets of 24, 27, 29 July and 1 August; the Russian Council of 24 July, which deliberated the Tsar's decision of 25 July) do not count as decisions.

Nature has no cadence (the timetable's steps can follow daily). The French voyage (23 to 29 July) is a guard part `in_paris` (`tick >= 31`), relaxable and named, not a cadence: a cadence is a refractory period after a decision and cannot express "not before a date". J7's build sets every k to 1 (`build(cadence=False)`), changing AUH (3 to 1) and FRA (2 to 1) only: by the record, the governments of Germany, Russia and Britain already decided on consecutive days when it mattered.

## D4. The timetables as pre-commitments

- Mobilization levels are orders; nothing lowers them except `RUS.revoke`, available from `pre_mobilization` or `partial` until general mobilization. The record's revocation of 29 July cancelled the *general* order signed that day in favour of the partial one; the testbed's record line conflates the two (actions catalogue, `revoke`). The model keeps the architect's rule (revocation exists until general mobilization; the general order is irrevocable).
- **The coupling.** The German mobilization's effect includes the declaration on Russia (`GER.mobilize`). The architect's "guard part named `coupling` on the German mobilization's war effect" is written as the part `coupling` (constant FALSE) of `GER.mobilize_east`, the eastern-only deployment the Kaiser demanded on 1 August and Moltke refused because it no longer existed: the DSL has no parts on effects, and relaxing a part can only make an action available. Relaxing `GER.mobilize_east:coupling` gives Germany a mobilization without war, which is "the coupling relaxed" of J2.
- The General Staff's rule that Germany mobilizes when Russia does is the fairness `mobilization_race` (within 1 day; responses include `mobilize_east`). The plan's western steps are the fairness `western_timetable` (GER: the Belgian note and the declaration on France within a day each after the mobilization-with-war) and `liege` (Nature: the entry into Belgium the day after the note and the declaration on France are both in force). The catalogue gives every delay in days with its source.
- The Austro-Hungarian note's delivery date (19 to 23 July, Poincaré's departure) is a `delay` of 4, decided with the text: a pre-commitment, not a relaxable part.

## D5. Beliefs

`belief_GER_GBR` is raised or lowered by the dated events (Lichnowsky's telegrams of 29 July and 1 August as Nature events; Grey's statement of 3 August and the ultimatum of 4 August as effects of the British actions, clamped `Bump`s so that they compose with a Nature `Set` on the same day). It is read by no guard (the feasibility principle). The architect allowed a profile or fairness constraint named by J4 to read it; J4's claim names the restraint as being *in the decision space*, which needs no behavioural assumption, so none is declared, and the variable is the record against which J4's lag is measured.

## D6. Variables and endings

Variables as named in the testbed section 2, with the historian's levels (ontology). Two folds: `war_GBR_GER` is the expression `commitment_GBR == war` (the ultimatum expired the same day; no claim separates them), and the Belgian refusal has no level. One level added beyond the testbed's list: `ultimatum = resolved` (the Council of 7 July). "Great-power mobilization" is a mobilization order calling up reservists (ontology section 3). Endings: `GENERAL_WAR` (Britain in; any tick), `CONTINENTAL_WAR`, `LOCALIZED`, `NO_WAR` (at tick 38); other horizon states are `HORIZON`. Invariants for the kernels: `no_war_GER_RUS` (J2, J4) and `localized` (J3, J7).

## D7. Engines

The explicit engine fits: 181,913 reachable states from tick 0 over the four pairs (84,105 under `continental, fight`), explored in about 11 s; the J7 build 73,937. Exact kernels, attractors and pivotal coalitions therefore come from `wavefront.engine` and `wavefront.analysis` (`viability`, `point_of_no_return`, `distance`, `pivotal`, `sensitivity`); relaxation sets from tick 0 from the bounded checker (`wavefront.bmc.relax`, depth fixed by the claim), and from a mid-crisis state by the same encoding unrolled from that state (`models/ww2/bounded.py`, `relax_from`, imported unchanged); kernels under a relaxed assumption by recompiling with `relax=` and re-exploring. Every result names its engine. The "contested" reading of ww2_testbed.md section 4.6 applies where a claim says "contested"; none of J1 to J7 does.

## D8. The well-formedness check and the realized replay (before any claim)

`explore` raises on every ill-formed state (an actor without an available action, overlapping endings, two different `Set`s of one variable in one joint move, a monotone decrease), so a complete exploration from tick 0 is the well-formedness check: it passed for the whole model (four pairs) and for the J7 build, with no edit. The realized moves replay from tick 0 under `aims_RUS = fight` (both German aims) to `GENERAL_WAR` at tick 38, every row available; under `deter` the replay stops at tick 32 (the [H] gate of `RUS.mobilize_general`). This is J1's first check; J1 is also run as a registered test with the others.

## D9. Hold-out and the order of work

- Content, model, replay and well-formedness first; then J1 to J7 run **once, in one go**, the commit of first sight (`git rev-parse HEAD`, working tree uncommitted) recorded in the report and in `tests/july1914/test_model_1914.py`.
- **Total hold-out.** The architect names J2 to J5; the registered testbed (section 0) names J2 to J6 ("J2 to J6 are never re-run after a guard edit that follows their first sight"). The registered text governs: J6 is treated as total hold-out too. J1 and J7 follow the ordinary rule (a row in [guard_changelog_1914.md](guard_changelog_1914.md) with a source and a full rerun). After first sight, no guard, effect, delay, cadence, fairness constraint or profile changes for J2 to J6; a failing claim stays failing with its explanation and a proposed amendment for the owner.
- What was seen before first sight: the exploration statistics of the well-formedness check (state counts, and the count of terminal states per ending over the four pairs together: every ending occurs somewhere), the replay above, and nothing else. No kernel, attractor, pivotal coalition or relaxation set was computed before the run.

## R. Readings of the registered claims

Common to all: "under a hypothesis pair" means from the tick-0 state with both hidden objectives fixed (`models/ww2/hypotheses.fixed_model`); a "realized state at tick t" is the replay's state at t under that pair, built-ins included (pending counters, fairness and cadence counters), and exists only where the realized prefix is available. "Every coalition that excludes Russia" is every subset of {AUH, GER, FRA, GBR} (16, the empty one included), Nature always an adversary; the largest, **X = {AUH, GER, FRA, GBR}**, has the largest kernel, so its departure is the latest. A kernel's "departure along the realized path from tick t" is `t + point_of_no_return` of the realized states t, t+1, ..., 38 (the first one outside the kernel), `None` if none is outside.

**J1** (tick 0; explicit replay). Under each pair, the realized moves of `data/july1914/realized_moves.csv` are replayed with `cm.available` and `cm.step`. Holds iff under at least one pair every row is available at its tick (zero relaxations) and the play ends at tick 38 in `GENERAL_WAR`. Expected under `fight` (both German aims); under `deter` the stop at tick 32 is reported as the hypothesis's content.

**J2** (tick 32; explicit, from the realized state at 32). Under the pairs whose realized path reaches tick 38 (`fight`): (a) for every coalition C excluding RUS, the kernel `viability(g, C, "no_war_GER_RUS")` on the graph explored from the realized state at 32; the claim's first part holds iff every departure along the realized path from 32 is at most 34 ("within two days" of 30 July, inside 31 to 34). (b) The same with `GER.mobilize_east:coupling` relaxed (recompiled, re-explored from the same state): the shift is X's departure with the relaxation minus without it, a kernel kept to the horizon counting as departure 39 (one past the last state); holds iff the shift is at least 2. (c) "The coupling named as the driving variable": the named assumptions whose single relaxation puts X's departure state back into X's kernel (every assumption tried); holds iff `GER.mobilize_east:coupling` is among them. Reported besides: the forced and shortest distances (`distance`) at the departure state, the state-perturbation `sensitivity` at the state before it where there is one, the departure along the realized path from tick 0 (graph from tick 0), and the `deter` pairs for the state at 32.
