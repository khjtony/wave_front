How a guarantee zone is computed, and what makes it fast or slow

Three small working models. The first is the computation itself, on a world of 36 states. The second shows why the order of variables matters. The third shows what more cores buy. The real model has about 1025 states, but it runs exactly these steps.

1 · The computation: working backward, one quarter per layer

A toy world with two variables: society's reliance on AI (0 to 5) and oversight (0 to 5). It is safe while reliance is at most 4 and oversight at least 1. Each quarter governments pick one action (fund oversight, or cut reliance) and the frontier AI companies pick theirs at the same time (deploy, race, or both while oversight is 2 or less).

The question: from which states can governments keep the world safe whatever the companies do, for 8 quarters? The engine starts at the last quarter and steps backward. Each step is one layer.

A state drops out when every government action can be answered by a company action that leaves the zone. Nothing is simulated or sampled: each layer is computed from the one after it, for all states at once. In the real model one layer is a single very large operation, and there are 57 of them.

2 · Variable order: the same set, stored small or large

With 1025 states the engine cannot list a set. It stores it as a decision diagram: a chain of yes/no questions about single bits, with identical sub-chains shared. How much sharing is possible depends on the order in which the bits are asked.

The toy set below says "some player's action bit and the state bit it acts on are both on" (four such pairs). It is the same set in every order. Only the size of its diagram changes.

This is what happened in the real engine. Its order kept every player's action bits at the top, far from the state bits they change. Moving them next to those bits made zones on the smaller models more than 20 times cheaper. On the full model the best order found so far gives 1.3 to 1.8 times, because the search for a good order stalls.

3 · More cores: where the time goes

Inside one layer, the big step is a substitution done node by node, level by level. The nodes of one level do not depend on each other, so several cores can work on a level at once. But parallel workers repeat some of each other's work, and every memory clean-up empties their shared notes.

The numbers behind this toy are measured: the multi-core engine on a zone of AGI v0.3 with race pressure, 23 November 2026. On the largest zone of the full model the gain at 12 threads fell to about 1.5 times, for six times the processor time.
