Open weights: what the model assumes, and what the world looks like

The owner's point: good open-weight models already exist today. The research confirms it and sharpens it. In the model an open-weight release is a single event that has not happened yet. In the world, a downloadable model has trailed the best closed one by about four months for nearly two years, and the best closed model has never been released. The model should carry a gap, not a switch.

This page sets the model's open-weight assumptions against a web survey of 18 November 2026 (docs/agi_open_weights_research_2026-11-18.md, with a verification label on every fact). Nothing here has been changed in any model. Section 4 lists what the owner would decide.

3 · What the model has said about open weights so far

  • Retraining is the consequence that removes guarantees (E6): from an aligned start a silent takeover becomes possible in 7 quarters, and governments alone lose the coexistence guarantee under every starting objective.
  • With every consequence on, the starting objective stops mattering for how fast a takeover can come: 7 quarters from all four (E5's bounded part).
  • Race pressure changes no guarantee (E3 (b)); no recall and outside testing change none of those tested (E2, E4 (b)).
  • In random plays of v0.2 a silent takeover ends 4.0% of plays, against 0.054% in v0.1, and the same from an aligned start as from a self-preserving one.
  • The labs' own release barely matters in v0.1: removing it changes no fastest road (racing substitutes), and moving its effort score moves the censuses by about a point.
  • Still open: the overall balance (E5), which needs the memory upgrade.

Put beside section 2: the model's sharpest open-weight result rests on the factor with the best evidence (D), and its weakest factor (B) turned out not to matter. That is reassuring about the results so far. The flag itself is the part that is wrong.

4 · What the owner would decide

  1. Replace the switch with a gap. Proposed: a variable for how many quarters the best open system trails the labs' system (none open; 3 or more; 1 to 2; level), starting at 1 to 2. The consequences then apply to the open system at its capability, so retraining an open system matters only once the open system is itself at the level in question. This is a new model version.
    A cheaper first step: keep the switch and add "already open at the start" as an open call of the snapshot, run beside "closed at the start".
  2. Extend "no recall" to shutdown? The evidence supports it. On 17 November the owner chose the pause rule only.
  3. Race pressure: keep as it is, re-specify, or drop. Proposed: drop it from the next version; it is weakly supported and changes nothing.
  4. Outside testing: proposed: keep faster evaluation and detection, remove faster oversight-building.
  5. Retraining: keep. Proposed: drop the direction toward aligned, for which nothing was found.
  6. The second system (model v1): move it up. It is the faithful form of both retraining and copies outside the labs.
  7. A wording correction in our own documents. The June 2026 action against Fable 5 was a licence requirement for access by foreign persons (an "Is-Informed" letter), not an order to suspend the models; the lab disabled them worldwide because it could not check nationality. docs/agi_v0_1.md and docs/agi_delays_2026-11-14.md describe it more loosely. The conclusion drawn from it (a halt within days on a hosted model) stands.

How far to trust the survey

  • It was done by web search on 18 November 2026, and most of the 2026 facts are newer than anything I can check independently.
  • The strongest sources are Epoch AI, the UK and US safety institutes, the White House plan and one law paper read as raw text. Several news pages refused the fetch and are known only from search summaries; the note marks each.
  • Quotes labelled "verified" passed through a summarising tool. They should be checked against the page before being quoted outside this project.
  • Public benchmarks probably understate the gap: closed labs do not always release their best model, and open models do worse on private tests.
