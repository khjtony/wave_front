# Review of the goal and the design, 2026-11-09

Status: written on the owner's request of 2026-11-09 ("check if the goal is valid, and if existing design aligns with the goal and is optimal; what would you do differently from scratch"), at `main` 6c5108b. It rests on the design and status documents and on spot checks of the Cold War model, not on a code audit. It edits no registered claim. The owner's decision on its recommendation is section 9 of [owner_decisions_2026-10-23.md](owner_decisions_2026-10-23.md).

**Verdict.** The goal is valid in a narrowed form and the method fits it, but the design is not optimal. After 140 commits there is no AGI model, and most of the effort went into historical validation that cannot validate the AGI-specific content and has mostly failed on its own terms.

## 1. Is the goal valid?

- **Valid as:** "under which stated assumptions, and with which coalitions, does coexistence hold or fail". Reachability instead of forecasting is the right choice for entities with no precedent.
- **Not valid as:** "validated by history" or "supplies strategies" without qualification. [validity.md](validity.md) section 6 lists what history leaves untested: recursive capability growth, a copyable actor, lock-in with no adversary, and speed and information asymmetry. That list is the AGI part. Outputs will always be conditional on a model nobody can validate, and the goal statement should say so.

## 2. Does the design align, and is it optimal?

The deliverables fit the goal: minimal assumption sets, kernels and pivotal coalitions. Registering claims before guards and leaving failures standing is the project's strongest asset. The problems:

- **Sequencing.** G1 to G22 are registered but never run. The evidence pipeline is half of `wavefront` and serves the historical replays. The toy AGI scene answers in seconds and already gave the most goal-relevant findings.
- **Validation failed.** C2 to C11 fail on Cold War v0, and V2, V3 and V5 to V9 fail on v1. Transfer hypotheses H1, H2, H4 and H7 failed or are unsupported, and many WW2 passes are "as amended". Expanding the Cold War strategy space now (decision 8h) is fitting to the validation set.
- **Failures that are semantics, not history.** A conjunct one side cannot defend empties its kernel, beliefs cannot move joint kernels, and "contested" vanishes in dyads. A toy model shows each of these in a day.
- **No incentives.** The framework models ability only. Deterrence is written into guards (`invade_west` requires `nuke_USA == "none"`), so it is assumed, not derived. That is why H7 fails, and it matters more for AGI, where diplomacy means changing incentives.
- **Perfect-information strategies.** Hidden objectives are the core of the AGI question, yet the solvers give humanity strategies that may depend on what it cannot see. Its results are optimistic bounds.
- **Single authorship.** One team wrote the levels, guards and predicates, and the engine is in-house. The independent cross-check is deferred to phase 6.

## 3. What a restart would do differently

- Build the AGI model in week one as a family of small variants, and make "which findings survive across variants" the headline.
- Use history as mechanism tests, with one small model per mechanism (belief lag, preventive defection, verification), not full-episode reproductions.
- Give actors ordinal preferences so that deterrence and commitment are derived.
- Make observation-based (uniform) strategies the default for humanity.
- Use an existing checker (MCMAS or PRISM-games) as the primary engine or as a cross-check from day one.
- Build predicate non-degeneracy, fortune variants and per-objective profiles into the framework as automatic checks.
- Keep the evidence pipeline minimal until the AGI model needs live snapshots.
- Treat every amended claim as a new registration, and get an outside reviewer for the decision spaces.

## 4. Recommended next step

Close phase 3 with the failures recorded as explained, write the disanalogies note, then build the AGI ontology and take first sight of G1 to G22.
