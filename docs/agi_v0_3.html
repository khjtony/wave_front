AGI v0.3: the open-weight system as a second AI

On the owner's decisions of 18 November the model goes straight to two AI systems: the labs' closed system, as before, and an open-weight system that follows it two quarters behind, is served by third-party providers, and cannot be shut down.

Registered 18 November 2026; nothing run. Claims F1 to F7 (section 5) and their test files (tests/agi_v0_3/scenarios/) were committed before any guard of v0.3 was written. The model is models/agi_v0_3/; v0, v0.1 and v0.2 results stand as computed. Sections 2 and 4 record the owner's decisions; section 3 gives the mechanics as registered

1 · The two systems

A · the labs' system (as in v0 to v0.2)

Closed weights

  • The most capable system; reaches each level first.
  • One of four hidden objectives, fixed for the whole play. The public cannot retrain it.
  • Works under oversight; its covert acts can be detected only where oversight exists.
  • A pause or a shutdown reaches it.
O · the open-weight system (new)

Open weights, served by open-weight AI service providers

  • Reaches each capability level 2 quarters after the labs, unless a treaty is in force.
  • Released with safeguards. Chance can remove them within the quarter and retrain it to any of the four objectives. Not safeguarded, it acts as that objective would.
  • Outside oversight, but open to inspection: its acts can always be detected.
  • No pause or shutdown reaches it. Governments can restrict the open-weight AI service providers that host it; they cannot reach private copies.

3a · Details fixed while building (after registration, before any run)

The table above left these open. Each was settled when the model was written, with no query of v0.3 run; none changes a registered claim or prediction.

  • Pressure is on from the first quarter. At s₀ both systems are pre-AGI, so O is level with the labs: the labs must deploy or race by the third quarter of every stretch in which either is available. It lifts while O is two quarters behind and returns when one quarter is left. Confirmed by the owner on 18 November, before any kernel claim was run.
  • A wave started by the labs and by the providers in one quarter is the providers' (one wave is pending at a time). The providers start one only when none is pending.
  • O's count is dropped when O is no longer behind (a shutdown lowered the labs' level). O can then stand above the labs' level, and the pressure applies.
  • An acquisition by O sets villain_seen, as A's do, so a slide during which O takes control does not read as DISEMPOWERMENT.
  • H.restrict_hosting has no precondition and no delay. H.deregulate lifts it together with its other effects (one regime level down, a pause ended).
  • Governments observe O's level and the hosting flag, not O's state or what it holds; labs do not observe O's state.
  • The refined builds of v0 (claim G13) exist without the open system only.
  • Effort scores: restrict_hosting has none yet; the least-effort queries of F1 run without the open system and do not meet it.

What goes away from v0.2: the single switch, the outside release as a chance event (the gap replaces it), retraining of the labs' own system (A is closed; O is what is retrained), and faster oversight-building.

6 · Cost and order

With everything on, v0.3 has several thousand times the states of v0.1 (O's level, its count, its five states, its control, the hosting flag, the providers' wave, the pressure counter, the pending treaty). v0.2 had twelve times and its largest kernel went over 26 GB. The kernel claims may not fit even 128 GB as registered.

  • Part A, at once: the bounded parts (F1, F2, F5). The bounded checker is not sensitive to the state count in the same way.
  • A timing probe of one kernel with the pieces switched on one at a time, printing time and memory only.
  • Part B: the kernel claims that the probe shows to fit, after the memory upgrade. A kernel that does not fit reads not computed; simplifying the model to make it fit is a new version.
