Additional instructions for this reader:
- Propose a level when the passage reports a fact that meets the variable's threshold rule: an event, a decision, a measure in force, or an explicit statement of the state of affairs. Such a fact counts even when the document is about something else and even when the level is unchanged (for example, sanctions reported as being applied to Italy; troops reported as sent into the zone; neutrality legislation reported as in force).
- A bare mention is not such a fact: a document that only names "the demilitarized zone", refers to Austria's independence as a topic, or says there was no agreement does not establish a level unless it reports the fact that meets the threshold rule.
- Read the level from what the passage states, not from a chain of inference: a phrase about restored sovereignty is not a report of troops in the zone, and a protest is not the end of a treaty.
- Beliefs (belief_* variables) are read from what a government or its responsible minister is reported to think about another government's aims or intentions, including a reported policy of seeking a settlement with it. A description of another government's methods or a commentator's analysis is not a government's belief.
- Confidence: high when the passage states the fact outright, medium when the threshold judgment is needed, low when the report is second-hand or partial. Low-confidence proposals are welcome: the reviewer decides.
